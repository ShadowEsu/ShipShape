# Make ShipShape Read Like a Trusted Ledger

ShipShape should look and behave like a calm, well-edited newspaper about the user's own business. Every form, fact and deadline should carry a source and a date, every AI action should be visible and checkable, and nothing should claim more than the product can prove. The design language can capture the "Claude plus New York Times" feel without borrowing either brand's assets. Both rely on proprietary typefaces, but what makes them feel the way they do is a set of portable editorial conventions. The open-license recipe is Newsreader for headlines and reading text, IBM Plex Sans for the interface, IBM Plex Mono for form numbers, near-black ink on neutral (not cream) paper, a single navy accent, hairline rules, datelines and a right-hand "margin notes" rail. The app should copy the simplest pattern that today's AI and finance tools have converged on: one "New task" composer, five fixed destinations and a command palette. The four modes (Startup, Small business, Freelancer, Contractor/Insurance) work best as an onboarding choice that seeds starter kits. They should not split the app into separate surfaces. Most of the AI's speed comes from *not* calling a large model. The design fills fields deterministically from confirmed facts, uses Claude Haiku 5.5 for field mapping, caches prompts, streams drafts, and uses Sonnet or Opus 5.5 only for prose. The founder's "70% complete" idea needs care. Research on progress bars is mixed to negative: they help only when progress looks faster than expected and hurt when it looks slow. ShipShape should therefore count concrete checks ("14 of 18 checks done"), credit confirmed facts so users start high, show progress at section boundaries, and never display a business-wide "compliance" percentage. "Heavy warnings" should mean *specific* warnings at the moments that matter. Identical warnings repeated on every screen stop being noticed within days. The starter content also has a freshness problem: 2025–2026 rewrote much of it. BOI reporting is gone for domestic companies, the 1099-NEC threshold is now $2,000, Delaware's LLC tax is rising to $400, and a new ACORD 25 edition takes over after December 1, 2026. This report is product planning, not legal advice. A licensed attorney should review the drafting, suggestion and disclaimer designs before launch.

## An editorial system built from open fonts, not borrowed ones

**Claude's look and the NYT's authority both rest on proprietary type, so ShipShape has to rebuild the effect rather than copy the assets.** The studio Geist built Anthropic's identity around two licensed commercial families: Commercial Type's Styrene for headlines and UI, and Klim's Tiempos for reading text. The aim was to feel "technically refined and charmingly quirky" ([Geist](https://geist.co/work/anthropic); [type.today](https://type.today/en/journal/anthropic)). In late September 2025 a design studio credited a new set of bespoke "Anthropic Sans & Serif" typefaces by Chester Jenkins at BSPK ([DBCo on X](https://x.com/designbizco/status/1972759238049997006)). A secondary write-up describes them as Text and Display optical cuts. The Display cuts are sharper and higher-contrast; the Text cuts have a larger x-height and lower contrast for reading ([Gooova Studio](https://gooova.com/en/anthropic-designed-its-own-type-family/)). These faces are circulating on pirate font sites and unofficial CDNs ([fontiko](https://fontiko.com/fonts/444/anthropic-sans-font/)), but commissioned type is ordinarily proprietary, and one design-system index lists them as having no public web fonts ([shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/anthropic)). ShipShape must never ship them. The same applies to the palette. Anthropic's own public brand skill documents **near-black #141413 on cream #faf9f5 with a clay-orange #d97757 accent** ([anthropics/skills](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/brand-guidelines/SKILL.md)). That pairing is distinctive enough that copying it would read as a Claude knockoff. What ShipShape can legitimately take from Claude is the underlying grammar: a serif for reading paired with a quiet sans for controls, a paper-like ground, one sparingly used accent, a narrow reading measure and generous whitespace.

The New York Times works the same way. Its typefaces are custom cuts commissioned by design director Tom Bodkin, largely drawn by Matthew Carter and Font Bureau / Carter & Cone ([Luc Devroye](https://luc.devroye.org/fonts-26319.html)). Each face has a strict role. Cheltenham sets news headlines. Franklin sets "meta information, captions, and navigation." Stymie and Karnak set louder feature headlines. Body text has been Georgia or Imperial ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/3907/the-new-york-times-article-redesign-may-2013)). The authority comes from that **role discipline**: kicker over headline over dek over byline, hairline rules instead of boxes, black-on-white restraint with color reserved for meaning, and datelines that signal the content is current. Those conventions are generic and free to use. For a paperwork product, the dateline is the single most valuable thing to borrow. A line such as "Form W-9 · IRS · Rev. March 2024 · checked 12 Oct 2026" turns a newspaper habit into a trust device, and it should appear on every form, fact and resource.

### The type stack

Every candidate face below was confirmed as OFL-licensed in the google/fonts repository metadata ([google/fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl)). **Newsreader** is the lead recommendation. Google Fonts commissioned it from Production Type specifically "for on-screen, longer-form reading" ([Production Type](https://productiontype.com/family/newsreader)). Its optical-size axis runs from 6 to 72, so a single family behaves like Text cuts at 13px captions and Display cuts at 72px headlines. That is the same Text/Display logic Anthropic paid to have built. **IBM Plex Sans** handles interface text and labels. It is engineered and neutral, it has a width axis for dense tables, and it avoids the "AI-generated site" signal that Inter now carries. **IBM Plex Mono** sets form numbers, EIN masks, passport IDs and the audit log. **Source Serif 4** (opsz 8–60) is the sturdier fallback serif, and Stanford uses it as its primary institutional face ([Stanford Identity](https://identity.stanford.edu/design-elements/typography/primary-typefaces/)). **Public Sans**, built for the US Web Design System, is an alternate UI face if the team wants a subtle "government form" cue. Skip Fraunces, which reads as playful, and Instrument Serif, which has no text cut and breaks down below about 28px.

| Role | App | Marketing | Face |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kicker / label | 12px, 600, uppercase, +0.06em | 13px | IBM Plex Sans |
| Caption / meta / dateline | 12/16 | 13/18 | IBM Plex Sans |
| UI body, controls, tables | 14/20, tabular numerals | — | IBM Plex Sans |
| Reading body (drafts, guides) | 16/26 | 18/30 | Newsreader, opsz auto |
| H3 | 20/28, 600 | 28/34 | Newsreader |
| H2 | 24/30, 600 | 40/46 | Newsreader |
| H1 | 32/38, 600 | 56–72, line-height 1.05, −0.01em | Newsreader |
| Form IDs, EIN, passport ID | 13–14px | 14px | IBM Plex Mono |

The app scale steps by about 1.25 and the marketing scale by about 1.333. Use `text-wrap: balance` on headlines and `text-wrap: pretty` on paragraphs. Self-host subsetted variable fonts with metric-matched fallbacks to protect layout stability, and keep the license files in the repository.

### Palette, contrast and status

Roughly 90% of any screen should be ink, paper and gray. The navy accent is reserved for links and primary buttons. Status colors always come with an icon and a text label, because WCAG 2.2 forbids color as the only carrier of meaning ([W3C SC 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)). Form-field borders and focus rings need 3:1 against their surroundings ([W3C SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)), so the light decorative rule color can never serve as an input border. The ratios below were computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula against the 4.5:1 text minimum ([W3C SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)).

The paper is deliberately neutral off-white rather than Anthropic's cream, and the accent is navy rather than clay. Dark mode is a separate token set, not an inversion. Document previews stay on a white "sheet" even in dark mode, because the documents are white in real life.

### Grid, components and motion

On the marketing site, use a 12-column grid at 1200–1280px with 24px gutters, 64–120px section spacing and a 60–72 character reading column. Prefer asymmetric 4/8 splits, with the kicker and dateline in the left rail, over a centered hero with three cards. Separate sections with 1px rules instead of shadowed cards. Corner radius is 4–6px, and elevation comes from borders, not shadows.

The app layout is the editorial signature. A left navigation rail sits beside a central "document sheet" on a gray ground, and a right **margin-notes rail** carries warnings, citations, provenance and AI explanations, set like footnotes. The core component set is small: a kicker label, a dateline and source line, an "Editor's note" warning box with a left ink rule, footnote-numbered citations, provenance chips ("From your profile, confirmed 2 Sep 2026"), a check-answers summary, and a printable passport "dossier" page with a serif title, a mono ID and line-art status seals. The seals use generic stamp and passport conventions, never a real government design or agency seal. Motion is functional only: 150–250ms ease-out, 4–8px panel slides, a roughly 400ms highlight sweep when a PDF field fills, and nothing at all when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set. For imagery, use real product screenshots set like magazine plates with captions, plus one-color engraving-style spot illustrations of stamps, folders and ledgers.

### What not to copy, and why it matters legally

The hard legal line is fonts. Shipping NYT or Anthropic font files, including "free" leaked copies, is infringement. A softer but real line is trade dress. In *Ingrid & Isabel v. Baby Be Mine*, a court let a website's "look and feel" proceed as protectable trade dress where similar fonts, colors and layout were alleged, and intentional copying can help prove secondary meaning ([AIPLA](https://www.aipla.org/innovate/does-this-web-dress-make-my-site-look-distinctive); [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/images/content/9/7/v2/977/EcommerceLawReports-GhajarLevineNovDec2014.pdf)). There is even precedent for a look-and-feel suit between two legal-document preparation services ([IP Trademark Attorney](https://www.iptrademarkattorney.com/web-site-trade-dress-look-and-feel-copyright-legal-document-preparation-help/)). These are mostly district-court rulings, so the risk is moderate rather than certain. It is still avoidable at no cost. ShipShape should stay away from a blackletter wordmark, a "T"-like monogram, a "Times"-style masthead and Cheltenham-plus-Franklin in NYT proportions on one side, and from cream with clay orange or a slash, asterisk or spark glyph as a logo on the other. FT salmon, "Claude" or "Times" puns and any implied partnership are equally off-limits. It should also avoid the generic AI-site look that undercuts "super professional": purple-to-blue gradient heroes, Inter everywhere, pill badges, three icon cards, neon glow, sparkle "AI" icons and emoji used as icons ([robotsonpayroll](https://robotsonpayroll.substack.com/p/how-to-avoid-that-vibe-coded-look); [prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). One useful check is the "reverse squint" test: ask a fresh model which brand or AI builder a screenshot came from. If it says Claude, NYT or "a v0 template," iterate. None of this system has been user-tested yet. Before committing, prototype it at 12–14px on Windows and Android and run a five-person preference test comparing Newsreader + Plex with Source Serif 4 + Instrument Sans.

## One composer, five destinations, and modes that seed rather than split

**The simplest credible shell is the one AI and finance tools have converged on: a slim left sidebar with one primary action, a few fixed destinations, pinned items and recent history, plus a command palette that reaches everything else.** As of July 2026, Anthropic was testing Projects in the Claude sidebar "above recent conversations," pinnable and sortable ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/anthropic-tests-new-placement-for-projects-on-claude-desktop/)). Stripe's 2024 dashboard redesign cut the number of sidebar links and added a "Shortcuts" area for pinned and recently visited pages ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/dashboard-update-may-2024); [Stripe Docs](https://stripe.com/en-pl/docs/dashboard)). This convergence is observed practice; no source measured sidebars against top navigation. ShipShape's sidebar should open with a **"New task"** button. It launches a single composer that can start any job: find a form, fill a PDF, draft a document, add a deadline or ask how something works. Below it sit five destinations: **Home** (the user's own front page), **Documents** (forms in progress, drafts and finished files), **Deadlines**, **Business facts** (labeled "What ShipShape knows") and **Passport**. After those come Pinned and Recent, with inline rename and pin as in Claude. The resource Library, Settings, Billing and integrations sit at the bottom or behind the account menu. If a user runs more than one entity, a business switcher at the top of the sidebar beats nested folders.

The **⌘K command palette** keeps the visible interface small. Superhuman's canonical guidance applies directly: the same shortcut opens and closes the palette everywhere and returns focus to where the user was, matching is fuzzy so typos still find the command, every command displays its shortcut so users learn it, and aliases surface the preferred name ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/)). For ShipShape, aliases mean small-business vocabulary pairs such as "EIN" ↔ "Employer ID," "1099" ↔ "contractor form" and "COI" ↔ "insurance certificate." Most small-business owners will never press ⌘K, so a visible search field in the sidebar opens the same palette. For accessibility, use the ARIA combobox pattern, `aria-current` on the active nav item, targets of at least 24×24px, and sticky bars that never cover a focused field. These requirements follow WCAG 2.2 criteria 2.5.8 and 2.4.11 ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).

Home should read like a front page for the business, not a dashboard of widgets. It has three blocks: **"Due soon,"** with kickers such as "DUE IN 9 DAYS · IRS"; **"Needs your review,"** the queue of AI work waiting on the user; and **"Recently changed,"** covering rule changes that touch the user's tracked items, such as a new form revision. Each item carries its dateline and source. This is where the editorial design does real work, because the hierarchy of kicker, headline and dateline lets a busy owner scan the page in seconds.

### Modes as an onboarding lens, not a switcher

The four modes should be a **multi-select business-type lens** chosen at onboarding and editable later in Business facts. They should not be a toggle that changes the app's interface. Two lines of evidence support this. First, product teams add mode switchers only when they have genuinely different surfaces, as Claude did with Chat and Cowork ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/anthropic-tests-new-placement-for-projects-on-claude-desktop/)). ShipShape has one surface. Second, short persona questions that immediately tailor what users see are associated with activation gains. Make reported a 4–6% relative lift ([Candu/Make](https://www.casestudies.com/company/candu/case-study/make-boosts-activation-46-with-candu)). MYOB, an accounting product for small businesses and the closest analogue, reported **21%** from a baseline setup rate of about 14% ([Appcues/MYOB](https://www.appcues.com/customer-stories/how-myob-increased-new-user-activation-by-21-with-thoughtful-personalized-onboarding)). Guru reported 71% ([Candu/Guru](https://www.casestudies.com/company/candu/case-study/guru-boosts-activation-by-71-with-candu)). All three figures are vendor-published with no disclosed controls. They are directional evidence that personalization helps, not proof that asking a question by itself does. The selection has to be multi-select because real businesses overlap: a roofing contractor is also a small business with a local license, and a startup founder may freelance on the side.

The onboarding flow should be three skippable screens at most. The first asks **"What describes you?"** with Startup, Small business, Freelancer, Contractor & insurance and "Not sure yet," allowing several answers. The second asks **"Where do you operate?"** by state and city, because freelance-contract laws, local licensing and state annual reports all turn on jurisdiction. The third, **"Give ShipShape something to remember,"** invites a legal name and EIN or an upload of an EIN letter, COI or old W-9; extracted facts enter as *proposed* and must be confirmed before use, which shows the memory feature working from minute one. The user then lands on Home with a mode-specific first task. A freelancer gets "Create your W-9 send kit." A contractor gets "Upload your current COI." A startup gets "Start an 83(b) clock." A small business gets "Review your license checklist." Measure activation as the **first real artifact produced**, meaning a filled form, a drafted document or a confirmed deadline, not "profile completed."

The mode lens carries legal risk, and the framing of suggestions manages it. Courts have found unauthorized practice of law where software applied legal judgment to a user's facts. In *In re Reynoso*, bankruptcy software that chose exemptions and called itself an "expert system" crossed the line ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/477/1117/568640/)). The Supreme Court let the Second Circuit's broad reading of practicing law in *Upsolve* stand when it denied certiorari in March 2026 ([SCOTUS docket 25-948](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-948.html)). Mode-seeded content should therefore read as "Businesses like yours often track these," never "You need these." Each seeded deadline arrives as a suggestion with an explicit yes or no and nothing pre-selected, and declines are remembered.

## Speed comes from not calling the big model

**Users judge speed against three fixed thresholds, and ShipShape's architecture should be designed to each one.** Responses under about 0.1 seconds feel instantaneous. About 1 second keeps the user's train of thought intact. About 10 seconds is the limit of attention, and beyond it users need explicit progress and a way to interrupt ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). The Doherty threshold adds a practical rule: acknowledge every action within **400ms**, even when the full result takes longer ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/index.md)). NN/g's skeleton-screen guidance fills in the middle range. Show nothing for waits under 1 second, because a flash feels broken. Use spinners for single modules between 2 and 10 seconds, skeletons only for full-page loads, and an explicit duration estimate beyond 10 seconds ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)).

| Interaction | Budget | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Sidebar, palette open and filter, toggles | <100 ms | Client state, prefetch on hover, local index of recent docs and facts |
| Send a message or start a job | <400 ms visible | Optimistic insert plus a "Working" row naming the first step |
| AI answer or draft | First token <1 s | Streaming; plan shown first; pre-warmed cache |
| Form search | First results <1 s | Cached known forms instantly, live official results appended with source |
| PDF fill / long draft (10 s+) | Named steps, then background | "Found SS-4 on irs.gov ✓ → Mapped 14 of 18 fields ✓ → 4 need you," then notify |

**For forms, the fastest model call is the one that never happens.** Most popular official forms have an AcroForm layer whose field names, types and positions can be parsed in code. The recommended pipeline starts by **precomputing** field-to-profile mappings for the most-used forms (W-9, SS-4, state registrations) offline with the Batch API at 50% lower cost ([Anthropic Batch](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing)), stored as versioned mapping files keyed by form hash so most fills need no LLM call at all. Unknown forms get a low-effort **Haiku 5.5 mapping call** under a strict JSON schema that sees field labels and profile *keys*, never values. **Code writes the values** with a maintained pdf-lib fork, and only ambiguous fields **escalate** to Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5. Flat and scanned forms are the hard case. The FormGym benchmark found vision-language agents scoring **under 1%** end to end, because they could not locate fields. Adding a field-locating tool lifted accuracy by as much as **2% → 56%** ([FormGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14079v2)). So ShipShape should run a dedicated detector such as FFDNet (81.0 AP on its large model) and let Claude only *name* the detected boxes ([Hugging Face FFDNet-L](https://huggingface.co/jbarrow/FFDNet-L)). FormGym treats every form as an image, so it understates what native PDF parsing can do. That strengthens the case for parsing first.

Cost will not be the constraint; latency and accuracy will. Anthropic's October 2026 prices per million tokens are **$0.10 input and $0.50 output for Haiku 5.5** (prompts up to 100K tokens), $2/$10 for Sonnet 5.5 and $4/$20 for Opus 5.5, with cache reads at 0.05–0.1x of input ([Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). At those prices a cached-prefix Haiku mapping call costs well under a tenth of a cent per form. Caching is a prefix match in the order tools → system → messages. Stable content (system prompt, template, form schema) goes first and volatile content goes last. A `max_tokens: 0` request writes the cache ahead of time, which removes the cold-start delay ([Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). ShipShape should fire that pre-warm when a user opens a template, so the cache is hot by the time they finish the intake questions.

Drafting streams section by section into the editor. Three configuration details matter. Current models omit thinking text by default, which looks like a long pause before output, so setting `display: "summarized"` (or `"updates"` on the 5.5 models) gives the user visible progress ([Adaptive thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/adaptive-thinking)). Anthropic recommends `low` effort for classification and latency-sensitive routes, and because caches are scoped to a model, it suggests measuring the newest model at lower effort before building a multi-model cascade ([Effort](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)). A premium "fast mode" runs Opus 5.5 at up to 2.5x output speed for twice the price, on the Claude API only ([Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)). Anthropic publishes no time-to-first-token figures for these models, so ShipShape must benchmark its own routes.

### Making the AI *look* smart without overclaiming

The AI feels intelligent when its competence is visible. Before acting, it shows a one-line plan. During the job, it names which remembered facts it used ("Used: legal name, EIN, mailing address, all from your profile"). Each official form arrives with its dateline and source. The Citations API helps with grounding drafts in uploaded documents. It returns page-level pointers that are "guaranteed to contain valid pointers to the provided documents," but it **cannot be combined with structured outputs** and does not work on scanned PDFs without text ([Anthropic Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)). Field fills should therefore ground through ShipShape's own scheme. Every value is either `{value, source_profile_key}`, `{UNKNOWN, reason}` or `{NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, candidate, reason}`, and code rejects any value whose source key is not a confirmed profile fact. That makes "flag, don't guess" checkable by software rather than trusted to the model.

The eval set should hold 50–150 real federal, state and county forms paired with synthetic profiles that contain deliberate gaps. It should track a **hallucinated-fill rate** (target about zero) alongside a correctly-abstained rate. The reason for that strictness: even professional legal research tools were wrong 17% to 33% of the time in Stanford's preregistered study ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries)).

### Guardrails that live in code, not prompts

Four guardrails need to be enforced by code.

**Legal-question routing.** A Haiku 5.5 classifier routes every free-text question into product help, form definition, situational legal question, or other. Situational questions ("Should I…," "Is this enforceable…") get a fixed, non-generated reply that points to a bar referral service, SCORE or an SBDC, and every routing decision is logged. Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 can also return HTTP 200 with `stop_reason: "refusal"`, so the app must check that field before rendering any content ([Migration guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/migration-guide)).

**Uploads as untrusted input.** Prompt injection remains OWASP's top LLM risk for 2025, and documents are a named indirect vector ([Wiz on OWASP LLM01](https://www.wiz.io/academy/ai-security/owasp-llm-top-10)). ShipShape treats every uploaded PDF as untrusted data and gives the model no tools with side effects: it cannot send, sign or browse. Its only output is schema-validated mappings. Uploads that contain text addressed to AI systems are flagged to the user.

**Locked templates.** Templates are stored as locked segments plus named slots. The model sees and returns only slot contents. Code verifies a hash of each locked segment before rendering and again at finalization.

**Minimal sensitive data.** SSNs and full bank numbers never go to the model. ShipShape should request zero-data-retention where eligible, remembering that it does not cover the Files API or Batches ([Claude data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). It should also pin inference to the US with `inference_geo: "us"` at 1.1x price ([Claude data residency](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency)). This means the Batch API should be used only for mapping *public blank forms*, never for user documents.

## Count the checks, not the percent

### What the evidence says about "70% complete" bars

**The founder's instinct is half right.** Progress indicators help when progress looks faster than users expect and hurt when it looks slower. The best-known experiment, Conrad, Couper, Tourangeau and Peytchev (2010), found exactly that pattern. Progress feedback did not reliably raise completion overall and lowered it when progress seemed slow. *Intermittent* feedback kept most of the benefit while limiting the discouraging effect ([Conrad et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2910434)). Matzat, Snijders and van der Horst found negative or null effects among 2,460 panel respondents, depending on questionnaire length ([Matzat et al.](https://www.onlineumfragen.com/pic/matzat_progress_indicators.pdf)). A literature review finds positive, negative and null effects across studies ([IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/c8dd1de9-08e2-4bf3-a5f0-1e4e30207eb4/content)). A practitioner test at SurveyMonkey found that top-placed "percent complete" bars *lowered* completion in both short and long surveys, while bottom-placed bars helped by a few points ([SurveyMonkey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/progress-bars-good-bad-survey-survey-says/)). Most of this research is about surveys, and some of it predates 2015. No study tests progress indicators on AI-run tasks. The direction is consistent all the same: a raw percentage is a bet that progress will feel fast.

Two different kinds of progress are easy to conflate. **System progress** answers "how long will the AI take?" NN/g's guidance calls for a percent-done indicator on waits beyond about 10 seconds ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). ShipShape should honor that, but only for genuinely countable work ("Mapping field 14 of 32"), and otherwise show named steps. **Task progress** answers "how close am I to a finished document?" This is where the survey evidence applies. ShipShape can make the founder's "70%" honest by designing the denominator so that progress usually *does* look faster than expected.

| Item in a document | Counts toward "ready"? | What the user sees |
|---|---|---|
| Field filled from a fact the user already confirmed | Done from the start | "From your profile · confirmed 2 Sep 2026" chip |
| Field the AI inferred or extracted | Not done until confirmed | "Check: inferred from your uploaded COI" |
| Missing required field | Blocks export | One-question-per-screen prompt |
| AI-written span in a draft | Not done until Accept / Edit / Replace / Remove | Highlighted, labeled "AI-written" |
| Blocking warning | Not done until acknowledged | Editor's-note box above the action |
| Upstream change (profile, template version) | Reverts dependent items only | Meter drops, with a named reason |

Because confirmed facts count from the first second, a returning user's W-9 or vendor packet often opens at 70–90% and finishes in a handful of checks. That matches Conrad's favorable "faster than expected" condition. Present it as **"14 of 18 checks done"**, with the percentage as a secondary label. Use a thin bar segmented by form section, placed in the document header or margin rail rather than a sticky bar across the top. Update it at section boundaries rather than on every keystroke, following Conrad's intermittent-feedback finding. When something upstream changes, the meter should *visibly drop* and say why ("2 checks reopened: your mailing address changed"). That keeps the number honest, and it doubles as the re-review trigger.

**Never show a business-wide "you're 70% compliant" score.** A compliance percentage implies a legal judgment about the user's obligations, which is exactly the *Reynoso* problem. "Compliant" also belongs on the list of claims ShipShape should never make. The Home page can instead say "Your tracked items: 7 of 9 up to date," scoped explicitly to items the user chose to track.

### Review that gets faster with every pass instead of numbing

**The research on automation bias says repeated passive review produces rubber-stamping, not safety.** A systematic review of 74 studies found that incorrect decision support turned correct decisions into wrong ones in **6–11%** of cases. Users given wrong advice were 26% more likely to err (pooled RR 1.26, 95% CI 1.11–1.44). Emphasizing user accountability and showing confidence information helped ([Goddard et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751)). Parasuraman and Manzey concluded that automation bias affects novices and experts alike and "cannot be prevented by training or instructions" ([Parasuraman & Manzey](https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/items/0c787d91-6021-480b-a333-009e0d1d0092/full)). In a 199-person study, Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos found that cognitive forcing functions, such as making a decision before seeing the AI's answer, significantly reduced overreliance. Users nonetheless rated the most effective designs least favorably ([Buçinca et al.](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml)). Most of this evidence is clinical, aviation or lab work from before 2015, and no study tests repeated review passes on AI-drafted contracts. The practical lesson still holds: make review **active on a few high-risk items and fast on everything else**.

For forms, combine two well-documented GOV.UK patterns. Under "One thing per page," the user sees one question per screen, but only for missing or uncertain fields, which helps with unfamiliar processes and error recovery ([GOV.UK Service Manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure)). Then a "Check answers" page groups every value by form section. Each section has a "Change" link that returns the user to the summary afterward, skipped optional answers show as "Not provided," and the final button names the specific action ([GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/check-answers/)). Fields are grouped by risk. Low-risk fields such as phone formatting can be bulk-confirmed. High-risk fields need individual confirmation in place on the rendered PDF, each showing its source. High-risk means legal name, EIN/SSN, signatures, dates, amounts and checkboxes that assert facts.

For the two or three highest-stakes items, use light cognitive forcing. The user types the effective date or picks the paying party *before* the AI's value is revealed. Keep this to a few items, because users dislike it. Show confidence as categories ("Matched from profile / Inferred / Missing") that sort the review queue, never as percentages that suggest which fields can be skipped. Box warns that its own confidence score "is not a guarantee" ([Box Developer](https://developer.box.com/guides/metadata/confidence-level)). Returning experts and accountants get a "show all fields" single-page view, a merge GOV.UK explicitly allows for frequent users.

For drafts, use a three-way visual distinction: **locked licensed template text** in plain ink, **user-entered facts** in one treatment, and **AI-written text** highlighted and chip-labeled. Each AI-written span gets four choices: Accept, Edit, Replace with template default, or Remove. On every pass after the first, show a diff against the last reviewed version so the user reviews only what changed. A dependency graph resets to "unreviewed" only the spans and fields that depend on whatever changed, and confirmed facts stay confirmed across documents. That is how review gets *faster* with each pass instead of turning into a ritual. An optional maker-checker mode lets a second team member or the user's attorney approve through a share link. That uses the accountability mitigator Goddard identified. An exportable audit log records every generation (model, template version, time), every edit and acceptance, and every acknowledgment.

### "Heavy warnings" must mean specific warnings

Warnings habituate quickly. In a five-day fMRI study and a three-week field experiment, attention to repeated warnings fell over time, while **polymorphic warnings**, which change their appearance, substantially slowed the decline ([Vance et al., MIS Quarterly](https://misq.org/tuning-out-security-warnings-a-longitudinal-examination-of-habituation-through-fmri-eye-tracking-and-field-experiments.html); [Anderson et al.](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/1955)). GOV.UK reserves its warning component for genuine legal or financial consequences ([GOV.UK Warning text](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/warning-text)). One team moved its warning *above* the start button after finding that users don't read text below it ([DfE design history](https://design-histories.education.gov.uk/connect-families-to-support/giving-a-warning-on-connect-families-to-support-start-page)). So the founder's "heavy warnings" should be heavy in specificity and placement, not in frequency. The warning system has three tiers.

| Tier | When | Design |
|---|---|---|
| Blocking (rare) | Send, share, submit, anything irreversible | Editor's-note box *above* the button naming fact, form and consequence ("Filing with the wrong EIN can delay your registration"); specific button label ("Share passport with Acme Bank"). Because each names different facts, each is effectively polymorphic. |
| Inline flags (common) | Uncertain fields, AI-written spans | Small labeled badge beside the item, collected into a "3 things to check" list in the margin rail; no modals |
| Ambient provenance | Everything else | "From your profile" / "From irs.gov, Rev. 2024" chips with no warning color |

A permanent, quiet footer caveat handles the general "AI can be wrong" message. A generic banner should never repeat on every output. Reversible actions use undo rather than confirmation dialogs, since habitual confirm-clicking is well documented ([Andy Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zXH4ASvHNz488hCNgrBjQJK)).

Formal acknowledgments happen at exactly two moments. The first is before the user's first AI draft: ShipShape is not a law firm, and the user is responsible. The second is at finalization, with a list of what this user specifically confirmed ("4 AI-written sections and 3 inferred fields"). Each acknowledgment is a typed name plus a checkbox, logged with a timestamp and a document hash. Unfinalized exports carry a diagonal "DRAFT · AI-ASSISTED · NOT LEGAL ADVICE" watermark.

No US law currently requires machine-readable marking of AI-written *text*. California's SB 942 covers image, video and audio from providers with more than 1 million users, and AB 853 delayed it to August 2, 2026 ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab853); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/californias-ongoing-ai-regulation-key-deadlines-arriving-2026-and-beyond)). The EU AI Act's Article 50 marking duties matter only if ShipShape targets EU users ([Bristows](https://inquisitiveminds.bristows.com/post/102nfaz/the-european-commissions-code-of-practice-on-transparency-of-ai-generated-conten)). Geo-limiting to the US at launch defers that question. The labeling regime above is therefore a voluntary trust and liability choice.

Sharing a passport is the one moment where full review earns its friction. The share screen shows exactly what the recipient will see, lets the user include or exclude each section, and sets an expiry. It ends with a specifically labeled share button, and the link can be revoked instantly.

## Four starter kits, each rewritten by 2025–2026 rule changes

**Mode content is where ShipShape's credibility will be won or lost. Much of it changed in 2025–2026, and stale content is the fastest way to lose a user's trust.** Every starter-kit item below should ship with an official source link, a form revision date, a "last checked" date and a named reviewer. Each item should arrive as a suggestion the user accepts, not as an assigned obligation.

### Startup

The Startup kit centers on Delaware, equity and fundraising. Delaware corporations file their annual report and pay franchise tax by **March 1**. Late filing costs $200 plus 1.5% monthly interest. The minimum tax is $175 under the Authorized Shares Method and $400 under the Assumed Par Value Capital Method ([Delaware Division of Corporations](https://corp.delaware.gov/paytaxes/)). Explaining those two methods is a high-value, low-risk piece of content, because founders with large authorized-share counts routinely get alarming bills under the default method.

**Changed in 2026:** Delaware HB 400, signed May 21, 2026, raises the LLC/LP annual tax from **$300 to $400**. Because the tax is billed in arrears, the increase first appears on the **June 1, 2027** payment ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-state/delaware-increases-secretary-of-state-entity-fees-annual-taxes)). Most other Delaware filing fees changed on August 1, 2026 ([InCorp](https://www.incorp.com/resources/knowledge-base/delaware-business-filing-fee-increase)). The 83(b) election keeps its strict **30-day** clock with no late elections allowed. **Changed in 2025:** it can now be filed online through IRS Form 15620 with ID.me sign-in, which gives proof of receipt. That receipt is ideal passport evidence ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250724-say-goodbye-to-paper-cuts-section-83-b)). Practitioners report "quirks" in the online flow and warn filers to use only one method ([Daily Journal](https://dailyjournal.com/article/388813-83-b-goes-digital-quirks-founders-should-know-before-clicking-submit)). Form D is due within **15 days of the first sale** on EDGAR. Filing on time is not a condition of the exemptions, and newer filers need EDGAR Next credentials ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice)). The form itself was revised in February 2026 ([SEC Form D PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/form-d-02182026.pdf)).

**Remove all BOI reminders.** FinCEN's final rule, effective August 14, 2026, permanently exempts domestic companies and US persons. Only foreign-formed entities registered to do business in the US still report ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/es/insights/publications/2026/08/the-final-chapter-fincen-permanently-eliminates-boi-reporting-requirements-for-us-companies-and-us-persons); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260820-fincen-ends-beneficial-ownership-reporting)). A leftover BOI warning would be a credibility failure, so the kit should instead offer to record the company's exemption status, as practitioners advise ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/relief-is-real-but-not-universal-fincen-permanently-ends-boi-reporting-for-us-companies/)). The YC SAFE is © Y Combinator with no open license, so ShipShape should link to it rather than host or fill it ([YC Documents](https://www.ycombinator.com/documents)). Stripe Atlas is a useful structural model: it organizes founder documents by task (83(b), incorporation, equity, banking, taxes, SAFE) and states plainly that it gives no legal or tax advice ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas)). Several items remain unresearched: 409A refresh guidance, foreign-qualification annual reports by state, and SS-4 process details.

### Freelancer

The Freelancer kit is a W-9 send kit plus a tax calendar plus state contract checklists. **Changed for 2026:** the 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC reporting threshold, along with backup withholding, rose from **$600 to $2,000** for payments made after December 31, 2025, with inflation indexing from 2027 ([IRS Instructions for 1099-MISC/NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); [IRB 2026-19](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-19_IRB)). Any "$600" content for 2026 payments is now wrong. Industry summaries also report that the 2025 budget law restored the **1099-K threshold to $20,000 and 200 transactions** ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/)). That figure needs confirming on irs.gov before it ships.

Estimated tax payments for 2026 are due **April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027**. Self-employment tax is computed on 92.35% of net earnings, and the 2026 Social Security wage base is $184,500 ([IRS Form 1040-ES](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040es.pdf); [IRS estimated tax FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax)). The current W-9 is Rev. March 2024. It is a hybrid AcroForm/XFA file with comb fields and shared-name checkboxes, which the fill pipeline must handle explicitly ([IRS fw9.pdf](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw9.pdf)).

The location question at onboarding unlocks "contract must include" checklists. These are factual, not advice:

|---|---|---|
| New York State | $800 | August 28, 2024 ([Vedder Price](https://www.vedderprice.com/new-york-states-freelance-isnt-free-act-takes-effect-august-28-2024)) |
| New York City | — | May 15, 2017 ([Bond Schoeneck & King](https://www.bsk.com/new-york-labor-and-employment-law-report/new-york-city-law-protecting-freelance-workers-goes-into-effect-on-may-15-2017/print)) |

California's law requires a signed written contract kept for four years, and payment within 30 days of completion when no date is set ([Sen. Wiener](https://sd11.senate.ca.gov/news/governor-newsom-signs-senator-wieners-freelance-worker-bill-law); [4A's](https://www.aaaa.org/blog/new-california-freelancer-law-takes-effect-on-january-1-2025/)). The W-9 module stores the TIN once, encrypted and masked. It generates a signed W-9 on request and logs who received it, which feeds the passport.

### Small business

The Small business kit cannot be a national checklist, because the SBA itself defers almost every specific to state and local agencies. Registration, licensing and permits are set locally, and federal licenses apply only to regulated activities ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits)). The kit's value is a **jurisdiction-aware checklist where every row links to the issuing agency** and says "verify with [agency]." The core rows are DBA registration with the state, county or city, which most states require and which gives no legal protection by itself ([SBA](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/register-your-business)); state entity registration and the annual or biennial report; a seller's permit from the state revenue department, with renewals for regulated goods ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/node/2796)); a local business license; and, for restaurants and retail, county health and food-service permits, food-handler certification, alcohol licensing, sign permits, a certificate of occupancy and fire inspection. ADA content should link only to ADA.gov and never say "you are compliant." Building the 50-state table of annual-report and sales-tax schedules is a separate research and maintenance project, and it is the main content cost of this mode.

### Contractor & insurance

The Contractor kit revolves around certificates of insurance, endorsements, lien waivers and prequalification packets. **ShipShape should store, track, explain and request COIs, not generate or fill ACORD forms.** The ACORD 25 is copyrighted and licensed, and certificates are issued by the insurance agent. **Changed in late 2025:** ACORD published ACORD 25 (2025/12). Its license allows a replaced edition for no more than 12 months, so issuing the 2016/03 edition after **December 1, 2026** would breach the ACORD license. It would not be illegal ([Big I New York](https://www.biginy.org/news/ask-tim-news/the-old-acord-certificate-form-is-not-illegal-stop-using-it/)). That gives ShipShape a timely, factual explainer for the coming weeks: "Why your clients may ask for a new certificate after December 1."

A COI only *evidences* insurance and cannot amend the policy ([NY DFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2002/rg021204.htm)). The explainer must therefore say that the endorsement, not the certificate, controls. The common endorsements are CG 20 10 and CG 20 37 for additional insured (ongoing and completed operations), CG 20 01 for primary and noncontributory, and CG 24 04 and WC 00 03 13 for waivers of subrogation ([Jones](https://getjones.com/endorsements/general-liability/CG20101219); [NetVendor](https://faq.netvendor.com/article/219-common-industry-standard-endorsements)). They give ShipShape a vocabulary for extracting COI fields and auto-drafting a "please send an updated COI with these endorsements" email to the agent. Expiry reminders start 90 days out.

About a dozen states prescribe statutory lien-waiver forms, and in some, nonconforming waivers are unenforceable. They include CA, AZ, TX, NV, GA, MA, MI, MS, MO (residential only), UT and WY, with Florida's form optional ([Bradley](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2023/10/wait-is-my-lien-waiver-enforceable); [Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/lien-waivers/free-lien-waiver-forms/)). In those states ShipShape should offer the exact statutory text as a fill-in with a "must be used verbatim" note. Elsewhere it should use a generic template with a strong counsel-review warning. AIA and ISO forms are licensed families and should be linked, not reproduced ([NASBP](https://www.nasbp.org/post/the-aia-releases-sworn-construction-statements-lien-waiver-and-release-forms-for-the-first-time/)). Whether non-agent software may render ACORD forms at all is unverified and needs a legal read of ACORD's license.

## A library and a website that show their sources

### The resource library

**The library is the public face of the same dated, sourced content the app uses. It should be built as a form-guide collection with an editorial journal attached, not as an SEO content farm.** Federal works, including IRS, SBA, SEC and FinCEN content, carry no US copyright ([17 U.S.C. §105](https://law.justia.com/codes/us/2010/title17/chap1/sec105/)). ShipShape can quote and adapt them, crediting the agency as SBA asks and never implying endorsement ([SBA linking policy](https://legacy.sba.gov/about-sba/open-government/about-sbagov-website/linking-policy)). State content is not automatically public domain, so state forms default to link-out until the state's terms are checked. Legal and financial pages fall in Google's YMYL category, where accuracy and E-E-A-T dominate ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-search-quality-rater-guidelines-changes-july-2022-386815)). NerdWallet's small-business pages show what that looks like in practice: specialist bylines, published editorial guidelines, human fact-checking with "accurate as of" language, and a clear advertiser disclosure ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/reviews/gusto)).

Each form guide should follow one template, top to bottom: a one-line definition, who usually files it, the official source link and revision date, the deadline or recurrence, the fields explained in the agency's own instruction language, a dated "What changed" box, "When to ask a professional," a "Last reviewed [date] by [named reviewer]" line, the not-a-law-firm note, and a "Fill this with ShipShape" button that drops the user straight into the in-app flow. The language stays descriptive ("the IRS instructions say…"), never prescriptive. Pages auto-flag for re-review whenever the source form's revision date changes.

Freshness is the competitive edge. Payroll vendors published 1099-threshold explainers within weeks of the 2025 law and earned links for it ([Patriot Software](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/)). Year-specific titles such as "1099-NEC threshold 2026: $2,000" belong in the library. The Journal adds bylined, dated columns: Explainers, a recurring "Deadlines this month," Field Notes interviews with real owners, and Policy essays on privacy and AI. Inside the app, the same entries surface in the margin-notes rail next to the relevant form, filtered by the user's modes and state. Every "talk to a human" path points to SCORE, SBDCs or state bar referral services.

### The marketing site

The benchmark sites share one skeleton: a declarative hero, immediate proof, a product tour, a closing call to action, and a dense footer that always links Security, Status, Changelog, Help and Legal ([Linear](https://linear.app); [Mercury](https://mercury.com); [Stripe](https://stripe.com)). NN/g's trust drivers favor this structure. They are design quality, upfront disclosure (especially pricing), current and comprehensive content, and connection to the rest of the web ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Unbounce finds SaaS conversion rises with reading ease and falls with word count, against a 3.8% median ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). Anthropic's homepage, with dated announcement cards, sentence-case headings and a "Commitments" navigation group, and Stripe's "Book of the week" strip both show that editorial layouts can sit comfortably on a commercial page ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com); [Stripe](https://stripe.com)).

The home page should open with a one-sentence serif hero and subhead and two calls to action, **"Start free"** and **"See a sample passport,"** the latter a live passport for a clearly labeled fictional business. Directly beneath sits a short in-context line, "ShipShape is software, not a law firm. We don't give legal advice," placed the way Mercury puts "not an FDIC-insured bank" next to the claim it qualifies ([Mercury](https://mercury.com)) rather than in the footer-only position LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer use ([LegalZoom](https://www.legalzoom.com); [Rocket Lawyer](https://www.rocketlawyer.com)). The page then runs a three-part row (Remembers your facts / Finds the official form / Fills it, with warnings), real screenshots that *show the warnings*, a Passport explainer, a "Promises" block where each promise links to its proof page, named customers only once real ones exist, a pricing teaser with the actual price, a "From the Journal" strip, and the footer trust spine.

### Trust pages

Because ShipShape's positioning *is* privacy and honesty, the trust pages are product pages and deserve the same editorial typography as the home page. Anthropic groups its Trust center and Transparency pages under "Commitments" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com)). Linear's security page states concrete controls (TLS 1.2, AES-256 at rest) and gates audit reports behind a trust portal ([Linear Security](https://linear.app/security)). ShipShape's cluster should give **/security** the actual algorithms, US hosting, passkeys and 2FA, the staff-access policy, deletion timelines and a responsible-disclosure contact; **/subprocessors** a table of vendor, purpose, data categories, location and date added; **/ai** the model provider, the no-training commitment, retention at the AI provider and what the AI cannot do; **/privacy** a "short version" box above the full policy; and **/not-a-law-firm** a plain statement of what ShipShape is and isn't, with bar referral links. **/billing-promises** and **/accessibility** complete the set. Certification claims need discipline. The FTC's GoDaddy order shows that misstated security claims are themselves the violation ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/ftc-finalizes-order-godaddy-over-data-security-failures)). Until ShipShape has its own audit, it can truthfully say it is "hosted on providers with SOC 2 Type 2 reports" ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/security); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)). It must never show a SOC 2 badge.

### Pricing that proves "honest billing"

The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)), and the FTC reopened rulemaking in March 2026 ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/us-ftc-signals-renewed-interest-in-click-to-cancel-rulemaking)). Meanwhile, California's expanded auto-renewal law has been in force since July 1, 2025. It requires express affirmative consent, including for free-to-paid conversions, an annual reminder, and online cancellation for online signups ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect)). Adobe's March 2026 settlement of **$75M in penalties plus $75M in services** followed allegations of an early-termination fee disclosed only at cancellation ([DOJ](https://business.cch.com/ald/OfficeofPublicAffairs_AdobeAgreesto150MillionSettlementandInjunctiontoResolveAllegedViolationsoftheRestoreOnlineShoppersConfidenceAct_UnitedStatesDepartmentofJustice032026.pdf); [FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/032-RedactedComplaint-REPLACEMENT.pdf)). Rocket Lawyer's homepage shows annual plans as monthly equivalents that don't match its own FAQ prices, and its user counts differ between sections ([Rocket Lawyer](https://www.rocketlawyer.com)). That is precisely the kind of inconsistency NN/g says erodes trust.

ShipShape's pricing page should apply California's standard nationwide. Monthly and annual prices sit side by side with the annual total as the headline, and a "How billing works" box above the fold covers charge dates, renewal dates, reminders, two-click online cancellation, refunds and **no early-termination fees**. An unchecked consent checkbox sits next to the renewal terms, a reminder goes out before any trial converts, fake "Most popular" badges and countdowns are banned, and every number on the site draws from a single source of truth.

### Claims and proof

The FTC treats "AI" claims as statements that need substantiation, not as puffery ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-reminds-advertisers-to-keep-ai-claims-in-check)). It finalized its order against DoNotPay's untested "robot lawyer," with $193,000 in relief and a ban on lawyer-substitute claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)). Its 2024 rule bans fake and AI-generated testimonials ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). ShipShape should never use "AI lawyer," "legal advice," "lawyer-quality," "compliant," "100% accurate," "bank-level" or "end-to-end encrypted." It should prefer verifiable sentences such as "Finds the official form on the agency's own site and shows you the source," and "Flags problems before you file. You review and sign."

Texas offers a disclaimer-based safe harbor for software that "clearly and conspicuously" states it is not a substitute for an attorney ([Tex. Gov't Code ch. 81](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.81.htm)). North Carolina instead requires registration and attorney review of templates, and it bars standard liability caps ([N.C.G.S. 84-2.2](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_84/GS_84-2.2.html)). That forces a choice: register in North Carolina or geofence it out of drafting features. For drafting templates, Common Paper's CC BY 4.0 agreements are a ready licensed supply, used with attribution and with no implied endorsement ([Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/standards/)).

Performance and accessibility targets complete the professional feel. Core Web Vitals should be "good" at the 75th percentile: LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). The site should conform to WCAG 2.2 AA, even though the DOJ's Title II rule references 2.1 and applies to governments rather than private SaaS ([University of Arizona](https://accessibility.arizona.edu/policies-governance/wcag-22-highlights)).

## Conclusion

The research points to one organizing idea: **the dateline is the brand.** "Confirmed on [date]," "Rev. [date] from [agency]" and "Reviewed by you on [date]" serve as the editorial device in the type system, the grounding key in the AI architecture, the denominator in the progress meter, the trust signal on the passport, and the proof behind every marketing promise. Designing these as one system makes everything else coherent. Progress becomes honest because it counts confirmed, dated checks. Re-review becomes fast because only items whose upstream dates changed reopen. The passport becomes valuable because recipients can see freshness. The founder's "70%" survives in a better form: a count of real checks, which usually reads as faster than expected, rather than a percentage that promises compliance.

The largest ongoing cost is not the AI but the *editorial desk*. Much of this report's starter content changed in 2025–2026, and a single stale BOI reminder or "$600" threshold would undercut the whole trust positioning. ShipShape should budget for a named content editor, revision monitoring on every source form, and a rule that nothing ships without a source and a "last checked" date. The two failure modes to guard against are drift and premature promises. Drift means suggestions slowly turning into recommendations, or warnings turning into wallpaper. Premature promises means publishing promises or badges before the controls behind them exist. Before anything is built, the team should settle ACORD's license terms for software, North Carolina registration versus geofencing, IRS confirmation of the 1099-K threshold, the 50-state small-business deadline table, real latency benchmarks for each model route, and a small-size prototype test of the type system.
