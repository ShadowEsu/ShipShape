# Win Small-Business Paperwork by Keeping Promises

None of the competitors examined owns the small business's identity. They own a document, a signature, a vendor list or a formation filing. Nobody offers what ShipShape plans: a business profile the owner confirms and that carries across every requester, used to fill any official form, and shared through one live link. The incumbents make their money from recurring subscriptions with metered caps (Docusign, Dropbox Sign, Jotform), from annual lock-ins and trial conversions (Adobe, pdfFiller, Smallpdf, PandaDoc), and from compliance renewals and attorney upsells (LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer). COI and W-9 tools bill the *collector* per vendor and let senders submit for free. Users complain mostly about billing, not features. That pattern got Adobe a **$150M federal ROSCA settlement in March 2026**. On top of billing sits fine print about AI training that Docusign, PandaDoc and Jotform each word in ways that leave room to doubt. ShipShape can therefore build its position on promises it can actually keep. That means honest billing, a contractual "no training" pledge, deletable memory it confirms with the user, and granular, revocable sharing. Each promise has to be engineered first: application-layer encryption of tax IDs, a published retention schedule, US-pinned AI inference, and an audit log. The legal line that matters most is unauthorized practice of law. Courts protect software that acts as a scrivener, but they have punished software that applies legal judgment to a user's facts *even when the user clicked the final button*. So "the AI suggests, the user says yes" has to work like a search engine, not like an adviser. On the PDF side, the AI is not the hard part. Locating fields precisely is: frontier vision models score near zero there. The recommended stack is a maintained pdf-lib fork plus PDF.js, a purpose-built field detector (FFDNet) with Claude used only to label fields, Typst for generated agreements, and a PAdES seal plus an audit trail for signatures. This report is product research, not legal advice. Several legal citations come from primary sources that were not re-fetched in this research pass, and a licensed attorney should review the product before launch.

## Everyone owns a document; nobody owns the business

The PDF and e-signature incumbents all sell recurring subscriptions. Their headline price is the annual-billed rate, and the month-to-month rate is often about double. pdfFiller lists Basic at **$8/month billed annually** ([pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)), but the monthly price is about **$20/user** ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/reviews/162654/pdffiller)). Docusign Standard is about **$25/user annually versus $45 monthly**, with a cap of 100 envelopes per user per year. Its pricing page now leads with IAM plans "from $65 per user" ([PandaDoc blog, Aug 2026](https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/docusign-pricing/)). That source is a competitor, so treat these figures as directional. Caps are the main lever for pushing users up a tier. Jotform disables forms outright when a plan hits its signed-document quota ([usagepricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/jotform)). Dropbox Sign limits Essentials to **5 templates** ([eversign](https://eversign.com/blog/dropbox-sign-essentials-vs-standard-plans)). Those caps land hardest on ShipShape's target user, who sends the same packet to many counterparties.

The legal-document incumbents make their money from compliance renewals rather than from documents. LegalZoom reported **$756.0M in FY2025 revenue, about 65% of it from subscriptions** such as registered agent, compliance and attorney plans. Its ARPU of **$266** is under pressure from "a mix shift to lower-priced offerings" ([LegalZoom FY2025 release](https://investors.legalzoom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/legalzoom-reports-strong-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025)). LegalZoom's $249/year registered-agent service auto-renews. To cancel it, the customer must first appoint a new agent and file the change with the state ([LegalZoom RA page](https://www.legalzoom.com/business/business-operations/registered-agent-overview.html)). Rocket Lawyer bundles unlimited documents and e-signatures from **$149/year** and sells attorney sessions at $149 for 30 minutes ([Rocket Lawyer pricing](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/pricing)). That bundle sets a low price anchor ShipShape has to live with. Stripe Atlas (a one-time **$500** fee) and Clerky serve Delaware startups' formation and fundraising paperwork ([Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/stripe-atlas-review)), and leave restaurants and contractors alone. Common Paper and Bonterms give away attorney-drafted B2B contracts under **CC BY 4.0** ([Common Paper Standards](https://commonpaper.com/standards/); [Bonterms FAQ](https://support.bonterms.com/hc/en-us/articles/17912468621972-Standard-Agreement-FAQs)). That makes them a ready-made template supply rather than a competitor.

The AI form fillers come closest to ShipShape's memory feature, but they are built for professionals filling forms *for clients*. Filly offers reusable client profiles with a completeness score, color-coded confidence, and "Correct a field once. Never correct that form again." Its pricing runs from **$19 to $79/month**, and its target users are immigration lawyers, accountants and HR teams ([getfilly.app](https://getfilly.app)). Instafill restricts "custom profiles" to paid plans ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/instafill/what-is-instafillai-and-why-it-works-2jka)), but it publishes the best privacy disclosure in this group. It encrypts files with AES-256 before storage, uses per-workspace keys, sends LLM providers only extracted text and screenshots under no-training terms, and lets customers configure retention ([Instafill subprocessors](https://instafill.ai/features/subprocessors)). Simplicity AI advertises "100% accuracy," which no one has verified ([simplicity.ai](https://simplicity.ai)). None of the three asks the user to re-confirm stored facts that may have gone stale.

COI and vendor-compliance tools are where the money goes, and they all sit on the opposite side of the transaction. BCS is free up to 25 vendors, then **$0.95 per vendor per month**, billed to the client ([BCS pricing](https://getbcs.com/pricing)). Jones says it "never charges your vendors or subcontractors" ([Jones pricing](https://www.getjones.com/pricing)). TrustLayer launched a free tier in May 2025 for companies managing fewer than 50 vendors ([TrustLayer](https://www.trustlayer.io/resources/third-party-risk-management-for-all)). W-9 tools follow the same pattern. Tipalti, BILL, Ramp and Gusto each build their own vendor intake, so the sender re-enters the same data for every requester. A Gusto e-W-9 cannot be edited once signed ([Gusto support](https://support.gusto.com/article/100739998100000/Hire-and-pay-US-contractors)). KYB vendors such as Middesk sell to banks at a median of about **$11,875/year** ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/middesk)). No product in this research lets a small business carry its own "verified" badge.

Every reusable supplier profile found is locked inside a hub. Coupa sharing works only with linked customers who have enabled SIM, covers profile fields but not COIs, and requires sharing all sections at once ([Coupa Compass](https://compass.coupa.com/en-us/products/product-documentation/supplier-resources/for-suppliers/coupa-supplier-portal/get-started-with-the-csp/profiles/sharing-your-business-profile-information)). ISNetworld charges subcontractors **$875+/year** ([Billy comparison](https://billyforinsurance.com/resources/billy-vs-isnetworld-vs-avetta-subcontractor-prequalification/)). The sender-side tools that do exist are narrow point solutions. Constrafor's free hub handles COIs only, for construction only ([Constrafor](https://www.constrafor.com/the-build-up/did-they-get-it-ending-the-coi-submission-nightmare-for-subcontractors)). Certificial offers live COIs that update everyone a certificate was shared with, but the insurance agent controls what can be shared ([Certificial FAQ](https://certificial.com/faqs)).

## Billing traps and AI-training fine print are the category's open wounds

Billing is the most common complaint, and regulators have now attached a price to it. On **March 13, 2026**, DOJ and the FTC resolved ROSCA claims against Adobe for a **$75M civil penalty plus $75M in free services**. The government alleged that Adobe hid a "hefty Early Termination Fee" in "fine print and inconspicuous hyperlinks" and "thwarted subscribers' attempts to cancel." The order requires Adobe to disclose the termination fee before enrollment, send reminders before trials convert, and make cancellation easy ([DOJ release 26-252](https://business.cch.com/ald/OfficeofPublicAffairs_AdobeAgreesto150MillionSettlementandInjunctiontoResolveAllegedViolationsoftheRestoreOnlineShoppersConfidenceAct_UnitedStatesDepartmentofJustice032026.pdf)). The same pattern shows up elsewhere in consumer reports. Docusign users report being charged **$591.78** right after downgrading to free ([Docusign Community](https://community.docusign.com/esignature-111/i-was-charged-even-after-canceling-subscription-23641/index3.html)). pdfFiller users describe annual charges after cancelling a trial ([Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/pdffiller.com)), and a 2023 report says its Android app paywalls saving after the user has finished filling ([Deceptive Design](https://deceptive.design/articles/airslate-inc-pdffiller-requires-subscription-after-document-completion-and-free-trial-still-asks-for-cc-info)). Rocket Lawyer complaints follow the same lines ([ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/legal/rocket-lawyer.html)). The federal Click-to-Cancel rule was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025** ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)). ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws still apply, though, so honest billing differentiates ShipShape while also protecting it legally.

The second wound is AI training, where each vendor's wording leaves its own opening. Adobe says it "does not use customer content to train generative AI models" ([Adobe](https://business.adobe.com/in/products/acrobat-business.html)). Even so, its June 2024 forced terms re-acceptance set off a backlash that its own CPO called a matter of "unclear" wording ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2024/06/no-ai-training-in-newly-distrusted-terms-of-service-adobe-says)). Docusign trains on consenting customers' data after it is "aggregated and anonymised," and its page does not say whether consent is on or off by default ([Docusign AI Trust](https://www.docusign.com/en-gb/products/platform/ai/ai-trust)). PandaDoc's help center says customer content does not train any LLM ([PandaDoc AI policy](https://support.pandadoc.com/en/articles/13794735-pandadoc-ai-policy)). Its AI Addendum, however, claims a perpetual license and ownership over "AI Improvement Data" derived from AI output ([PandaDoc AI Addendum](https://www.pandadoc.com/legal/ai-addendum/)). Jotform's AI Policy says personal data is never used for training and also that form-field data "may be used to train" its AI ([Jotform AI Policy](https://www.jotform.com/ai-policy/)). pdfFiller makes no training statement at all. In December 2023, Dropbox turned on a "third-party AI" toggle by default for alpha users, then hid it ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/12/36298097/dropbox-ceo-denies-sharing-user-data-with-openai-but-the-option-to-disable-ai-search-is-now-hidden)). "We don't train on your documents" is now table stakes. A pledge without loopholes is not: one that covers the AI vendors too, has no exception for anonymized aggregates, claims no license over AI outputs, and keeps AI off by default. ShipShape can make that pledge because Anthropic commits that API data "is never used for model training without your express permission" ([Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)).

Security incidents involving third parties deserve their own attention. The **April 2024 Dropbox Sign breach** exposed API keys, OAuth tokens and MFA data. It also exposed the names and emails of signers *who had no account* ([Dropbox 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762324000024/may2024exhibit991.htm)). ShipShape's passport and two-way requests will hold counterparty data in the same way, so its security model and breach plan must cover people who never signed up.

## Store data so every privacy promise survives an FTC audit

The FTC does not punish weak security as such. It punishes the gap between what a company says and what it does. GoDaddy's 2025 order followed claims of "award-winning security" made while the company lacked MFA, and it bars misrepresenting compliance with standards ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/ftc-finalizes-order-godaddy-over-data-security-failures)). Blackbaud's order required a **published retention schedule that "precludes indefinite retention"**, including for former customers ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/05/ftc-finalizes-order-blackbaud-related-allegations-firms-security-failures-led-data-breach)). Drizly's order personally binds its CEO at future companies ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-takes-action-against-drizly-and-its-ceo-for-alleged-security-failures-that-exposed-data-of-2-5-million-consumers)). Chegg had to put an access and deletion link on its homepage and login page and offer users MFA ([King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/attachments/000/010/103/original/FTC_Signals_Increased_Enforcement_Effort_Against_Ineffective_Data_Security_Protocols.pdf)). Avast paid **$16.5M** for selling browsing data while promising privacy protection ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/06/ftc-finalizes-order-avast-banning-it-selling-or-licensing-web-browsing-data-advertising-requiring-it)). Taken together, these orders amount to a design spec. Write each promise as a narrow, testable statement, then build the control that makes it true.

**Architecture.** Supabase encrypts data at rest with AES-256, accepts only TLS 1.2/1.3, and holds a SOC 2 Type 2 report ([Supabase Security](https://supabase.com/security); [Supabase platform security](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/security/platform-security)). That is the baseline, not the promise. Supabase itself advises against its in-database column encryption "due to their high level of operational complexity and misconfiguration risk" ([Supabase docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/column-encryption)), and it labels pgsodium "pending deprecation" ([Supabase pgsodium](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/extensions/pgsodium.md)). The highest-risk fields are SSN, EIN, ITIN, bank numbers and signature images. These should get envelope encryption in the application tier: AES-256-GCM data keys wrapped by a cloud KMS, with the tenant ID passed as the KMS encryption context. Decryption then fails cryptographically if code asks for one tenant's data using another tenant's ID ([OneUptime KMS guide](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-02-12-kms-envelope-encryption/view); [AWS GenerateDataKey](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cli/v1/reference/kms/generate-data-key.html)). Store an HMAC lookup value and the last four digits alongside each encrypted value, so the UI, search and de-duplication never need to decrypt. The IRS sets a hard limit here. A person may not truncate its *own* TIN on a W-9 it furnishes to others ([IRS: Truncated TINs](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/truncated-taxpayer-identification-numbers)). ShipShape must therefore keep the full TIN for W-9s, which makes field-level encryption, masking by default, and step-up authentication for reveals essential. Row-level security is the single largest dependency. Every exposed table needs RLS keyed to organization membership, enforced by automated tests that attempt cross-tenant reads, with private storage buckets and signed URLs that expire in minutes.

**AI data flow.** Anthropic's privacy center says API inputs and outputs are deleted "within 30 days" by default ([Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)). A platform page says content is "not retained by default" except for Covered Models, which require 30-day retention ([Claude Platform](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). Disclosures should assume 30 days until a zero-data-retention (ZDR) agreement is signed. ZDR covers `/v1/messages`, including PDFs sent inline. It does **not** cover the Files API, Batches or code execution. Content flagged for usage-policy violations can be kept for up to two years even under ZDR ([Claude Platform](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). Setting `inference_geo: "us"` keeps inference in the US at **1.1x price**, and a workspace allow-list can enforce it ([Claude data residency](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency)). In practice, ShipShape should send documents inline rather than through the Files API, avoid Batches for sensitive documents, and mask tax IDs before any AI task that doesn't need them, such as extracting a COI expiry date.

**Hosting and law.** Vercel holds SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001:2022 ([Vercel compliance](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)). Its function body limit is **4.5 MB** ([Vercel limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)), so uploads go straight from the browser to storage and never pass through a function. Keep PII out of logs and add `no-store` caching on document routes. At launch ShipShape will fall below the CCPA revenue threshold of **$26,625,000** ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/california-ccpa-cybersecurity-risk-audits)). Breach-notification laws apply in every state from day one, however, and name plus SSN is a trigger in all of them ([NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/technology-and-communication/security-breach-notification-laws)). That makes an incident-response plan and cyber insurance launch requirements. A SOC 2 program costs roughly **$20K–$60K in the first year** ([TraceLayer](https://tracelayer.it.com/blog/soc2-compliance-cost-2026)). The sensible sequence is to publish a security page and subprocessor list now and start a Type I when the first general contractor, property manager or insurer sends a security questionnaire. Until ShipShape has its own report, it can truthfully say "hosted on providers with SOC 2 Type 2 reports." It cannot say "ShipShape is SOC 2 compliant."

Some terms should never appear in ShipShape's marketing. "Zero-knowledge" and "end-to-end encrypted" are false for a product whose server-side AI reads documents. "Bank-level," "military-grade," "unhackable" and "HIPAA compliant" are superlatives or certifications the FTC has already attacked.

## Stay a scrivener: the legal lines that keep the founder out of court

**Unauthorized practice of law is the existential risk, and a user's "yes" does not fix it.** Courts consistently protect software that acts as a scrivener. It can provide blank forms, let the user pick one, and record the user's own facts. Liability attaches when the software or its staff applies legal judgment to the user's situation. In *Janson v. LegalZoom* (W.D. Mo. 2011), the problem was a branching questionnaire plus employee review that turned answers into a finished legal document ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/?q=Janson+v.+LegalZoom)). In *In re Reynoso* (9th Cir. 2007), bankruptcy software that chose exemptions and called itself an "expert system" was UPL, even though the user submitted the result ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/477/1117/568640/)). The law has not loosened since. In *Upsolve v. James*, the Second Circuit held in September 2025 that even advice limited to a one-page answer form falls within New York's definition of practicing law ([Public Citizen](https://clpblog.citizen.org/second-circuit-holds-unlawful-practice-law-can-apply-to-debt-collection-advice/)), and certiorari was **denied March 30, 2026** ([SCOTUS docket 25-948](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-948.html)). *Nippon Life v. OpenAI*, which seeks to enjoin AI legal assistance in Illinois, is still pending ([Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/nippon-life-v-openai-and-the-future-of-ai-legal-work)).

Two statutes give document software a safe harbor. Texas excludes forms and software from the practice of law if they "clearly and conspicuously state that the products are not a substitute for the advice of an attorney" ([Tex. Gov't Code ch. 81](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.81.htm)). North Carolina's N.C.G.S. 84-2.2 is stricter. It requires annual registration with the State Bar, NC-attorney review of every blank template, and a consumer-complaint path to the Bar. It also prohibits disclaiming warranties or liability, limiting damages, and requiring venue outside North Carolina ([N.C.G.S. 84-2.2](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_84/GS_84-2.2.html)). That conflicts with a standard liability cap. ShipShape will therefore need either an NC-specific carve-out in its terms of service plus registration, or a decision to geofence North Carolina out of template features. *Florida Bar v. TIKD* adds a structural warning. A non-lawyer company that positions itself between the consumer and the legal outcome is risky even when lawyers do the actual work ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/2021/sc18-149.html)). Any future attorney-referral feature should be designed with that in mind.

**Copyright splits by source.** Federal works, including IRS forms, are public domain under 17 U.S.C. 105 ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105)). *Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org* frees only works made by officials with lawmaking power, so state agency forms vary ([Supreme Court](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-1150_7m58.pdf)). The default for state forms should be to link out until the state's terms have been checked. Common Paper's standards are CC BY 4.0 and "free to use and modify" with attribution ([Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/standards/)). CC BY also forbids implying that the licensor endorses the user ([CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)). The YC SAFE is "free to download and use" but is marked © Y Combinator, carries no open license, and tells users to consult a lawyer. YC also now runs its own free "Send a SAFE" tool ([YC Documents](https://www.ycombinator.com/documents)). Linking to the SAFE is the safe default.

**E-signatures are valid for ordinary business contracts if the process proves four things:** intent to sign, consent to do business electronically, attribution to the signer, and an accurate record that can be retrieved later ([15 U.S.C. 7001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)). When a law requires written notice to a consumer, ESIGN requires disclosures before consent: the right to paper copies, the right to withdraw consent, the scope of consent, and the hardware requirements. Many signers are sole proprietors who may count as consumers. ShipShape should show the consent screen to every signer. ESIGN does not apply to wills and testamentary trusts, family law, court documents, or certain notices such as foreclosure and insurance cancellation ([15 U.S.C. 7003](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7003)). These categories, along with deeds and notarized instruments, belong on a hard blocklist next to ShipShape's existing exclusions for litigation and employment documents.

**The FTC polices AI claims and billing, not AI itself.** Under Chair Ferguson, the FTC set aside the Rytr order in December 2025 for "unduly burden[ing] AI innovation" ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/in-rare-move-ftc-sets-aside-rytr-order-for-burdening-ai-innovation-and-failing-to-plead-violations)). It still finalized the order against DoNotPay's "robot lawyer," which had never been tested against human lawyers, with **$193,000** in relief and a ban on lawyer-substitute claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)). It also ordered Workado in August 2025 to drop accuracy claims it could not support and to keep substantiation for future ones ([Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102l358/ftcs-ai-crackdown-continues-workado-ordered-to-drop-unsupported-accuracy-claims)). For billing, the FTC reopened click-to-cancel rulemaking with an ANPRM in March 2026 ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/us-ftc-signals-renewed-interest-in-click-to-cancel-rulemaking)). California's amended auto-renewal law has been in force since **July 1, 2025**. It requires express consent to renewal terms, including free-to-paid conversions, online cancellation without obstruction, an annual reminder, and consent records kept for at least three years ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect)). ShipShape should design to California's standard nationwide.

**Terms of service and entity.** Clickwrap holds up in court only with a conspicuous notice and an unambiguous action. *Berman v. Freedom Financial* struck down faint gray text ([9th Cir.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/03/30/20-16900.pdf)). California voids exemptions from liability for fraud and willful harm ([Cal. Civ. Code 1668](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1668)). It also refuses to enforce waivers of public injunctive relief ([McGill v. Citibank](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2017/s224086.html)). LegalZoom's arbitration clause pushed a 2024 New Jersey UPL class action into individual arbitration in 2025 ([Law360](https://law360.com/ip/articles/2326527&read_later=1&nlsidx=1&nlaidx=6)). A small startup, though, should have counsel weigh that protection against the cost of mass-arbitration fees. A Delaware LLC or corporation shields the owners from the company's debts ([6 Del. C. §18-303](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html)). It does not shield a founder named personally, as the Drizly CEO was. Tech E&O and cyber insurance are therefore the practical backstop. When buying a policy, confirm it does not exclude "legal services" or UPL claims.

## Build the PDF stack around detection, not the LLM

**Filling.** pdf-lib (MIT) is still the standard JavaScript AcroForm filler. Upstream, however, has not shipped a release since **1.17.1 in November 2021** ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/pdf-lib)). The maintained forks are `@cantoo/pdf-lib` (2.11.1, September 2026) and pdfme's `@pdfme/pdf-lib` ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@cantoo/pdf-lib); [pdfme README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@pdfme/pdf-lib@6.1.2/README.md)). Stirling-PDF has already switched to the Cantoo fork ([commit mirror](https://git.laurivan.com/Mirrors/Stirling-PDF/src/commit/0a1d2effdced141494a27691ab821391b4427086)). A hands-on test with the current IRS W-9 (Rev. March 2024) shows why the details matter. The file is a **hybrid AcroForm + XFA form with Reader-extension rights (/Perms /UR3)** and 23 fields. Its tax-classification checkboxes share one name and use export values /1 through /7. Its SSN and EIN boxes are comb fields ([IRS fw9.pdf](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw9.pdf)). pdf-lib filled and flattened the form correctly, but only after the XFA was removed. A name like "Zoë Łukasz" threw a WinAnsi encoding error until a Unicode TTF was embedded ([pdf-lib PDFForm docs](https://pdf-lib.js.org/docs/api/classes/pdfform)). The reliable sequence is:

1. Load the form.
2. `deleteXFA()`.
3. Remove `/Perms`.
4. Set each checkbox group by its export value.
5. Embed a font subset.
6. `updateAppearances(font)`.
7. `flatten()` for the clean download, and keep an unflattened copy so the user can re-edit.

One caution: the Cantoo fork failed custom-font embedding with the standard fontkit in that test. Pin a version and run the test suite against every fork upgrade.

**Rendering and in-PDF editing.** PDF.js (Apache-2.0, v6.4.299 published October 2026) is the active choice for rendering and editor overlays ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/pdfjs-dist)). Its built-in signatures are "simple annotations, not legally binding e-signatures" ([Mozilla support](https://support.mozilla.org/hi-IN/kb/pdf)), so signing has to be built separately. MuPDF.js is technically excellent but **AGPL**, which means a closed SaaS needs a commercial license ([MuPDF docs](https://mupdf.readthedocs.io/en/1.27.0/license.html)). PDFium-wasm wrappers such as `@embedpdf/pdfium` (MIT) are the permissive fallback if rendering quality falls short ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@embedpdf/pdfium)). Commercial SDKs buy support for dynamic XFA and high-fidelity rendering at a real cost. Nutrient averages about **$17.4K/year** for SMBs ([SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/nutrient)), and Apryse's median is **$25,140/year** ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/apryse)). Syncfusion's free Community License requires under $1M in revenue, no more than 10 employees, and **never more than $3M** of outside capital ([Syncfusion](https://www.syncfusion.com/products/communitylicense)). That last condition makes it a trap for a company planning to raise.

**Flat and scanned forms are where the AI earns its keep, and where it fails if misused.** The FormGym benchmark found **near-zero accuracy** for vision-language agents on end-to-end form filling, with field localization as the bottleneck "even in frontier models." Adding a dedicated field detector raised accuracy by up to **2% → 56%** ([FormGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14079v2)). The open CommonForms/FFDNet detector (WACV 2026) reaches **81.0 AP** for its large model and 93.5 AP on signature fields. It was trained for under $500 and ships with a CPU ONNX build ([Hugging Face FFDNet-L](https://huggingface.co/jbarrow/FFDNet-L); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16506v1)). The right pipeline has six steps:

1. Render the page to an image.
2. Run FFDNet in a worker or container.
3. Snap the detected boxes to the PDF's own vector lines.
4. Give Claude the page image with numbered box overlays, and have it name, type and map each box to profile keys.
5. Write real AcroForm widgets with pdf-lib.
6. Cache the field map by the form's hash so every user of that official form benefits. Cache field definitions only, never values.

Cloud OCR fills in for image-only scans; for example, Google Document AI Form Parser costs **$30 per 1,000 pages** ([Google](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/pricing)).

**Generation and signing.** Typst 0.14 produces **tagged PDFs by default, PDF/UA-1 on request, and all PDF/A parts** ([Typst changelog](https://www.typst.app/docs/changelog/0.14.0)), and it runs in JavaScript through WASM ([npm typst.ts](https://registry.npmjs.org/@myriaddreamin/typst.ts)). That makes it the best fit for clean, accessible Common Paper–style agreements. Headless Chromium on Vercel also works within the 250 MB bundle limit, but it is heavier ([Vercel limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)). For signatures, ShipShape needs three layers: a drawn or typed signature image for appearance; a server-side PAdES seal using a key held in a KMS, so later edits are detectable; and an appended audit page recording emails, IPs, timestamps and SHA-256 hashes from before and after signing. No 2026 JavaScript PAdES library was verified in this research, so that piece needs a technical spike. Single-signer flows such as W-9s can stay in-house. For multi-party contracts, the cheapest metered option is BoldSign at about **$30/month plus $0.75 per envelope** ([Verdocs](https://www.verdocs.com/blog/boldsign-pricing)). Documenso's hosted Platform plan costs $250/month, which avoids taking on AGPL obligations by self-hosting it inside the product ([Documenso pricing](https://documenso.com/pricing)).

**Upload safety.** Use signed direct uploads into a quarantine bucket. A background job then caps file size and page count, rejects encrypted or malformed files, and strips `/JavaScript`, `/OpenAction`, `/Launch` and embedded files ([Swifdoo](https://www.swifdoo.com/blog/remove-active-content-from-pdf)). Keep PDF.js scripting off, and serve PDFs from a separate origin.

## Promises ShipShape can make on its website and in the app

Every promise below is backed by a documented competitor failure or a regulatory benchmark. Each also depends on an engineering or operational control. Before the control is live, the promise is a liability under FTC Act Section 5, so publish it only once the condition in the last column is true.

|---|---|---|
| "Cancel online in two clicks, anytime. No early-termination fees, ever." | Adobe's $150M ROSCA settlement; CA auto-renewal law requires online cancellation | Self-serve cancel with no forced retention chat; consent logs kept 3+ years |
| "We email you before any trial converts or any annual plan renews, with the amount and a cancel link." | Smallpdf and pdfFiller trial-to-annual complaints; Adobe order's reminder requirement | Automated reminders; annual reminder per CA law |
| "One price, shown monthly and yearly side by side." | Competitors' month-to-month prices are often ~2x the advertised annual rate | Pricing page parity |
| "Free means you can fill, sign and download your finished PDF. No card required." | pdfFiller's reported paywall after completion | Free tier includes export |
| "We never use your documents or business profile to train AI. Neither do our AI providers. No 'anonymized' exception, and we claim no rights over AI output." | Docusign consent-to-train, PandaDoc AI Addendum, Jotform contradictions; Anthropic's no-training commitment | Anthropic commercial terms confirmed; every AI subprocessor listed with matching terms; no training pipeline exists |
| "AI features are off until you turn them on, and you can switch them off for any single document." | Dropbox's default-on toggle in 2023 | Workspace and per-document AI toggles enforced server-side |

| "Billing help for everyone, including free and cancelled accounts." | Docusign's support paywall for free users | Staffed billing channel |

Several phrases should never appear anywhere in ShipShape's copy:

- Legal and AI claims: "AI lawyer," "legal advice," "lawyer-quality," "attorney-approved" (unless documented under 84-2.2), "compliant," "legally guaranteed."
- Security claims: "bank-level," "military-grade," "zero-knowledge," "end-to-end encrypted," "unhackable," "HIPAA compliant."
- Accuracy claims: any accuracy percentage without a substantiation file.

"No per-envelope caps on your own packet" is attractive given Docusign's and Jotform's caps. Promise it only after modeling the cost of the e-sign API, because a promise that later has to be withdrawn is worse than never making it.

## Product rules for AI suggestions and memory re-confirmation

The suggestion engine must behave like a search engine for public paperwork, never like an adviser. Liability in *Reynoso* and *Janson* turned on applying judgment to the user's facts, and *Upsolve* shows that even help with a single form counts as legal practice. The memory system has a matching job: it must be a dated record of facts the owner has confirmed, never an inference engine. Together these rules make ShipShape's AI safe to sell.

| Area | Rule | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Suggestion framing | Show several results ranked by text relevance, never one "answer." Use the wording "Forms matching 'contractor' and 'invoice'" or "Businesses searching for X often look at these official forms." Never "you need," "the right document for you," "based on your situation we recommend," "this protects you," or "compliant." | Avoids personalized legal judgment (*Reynoso*, *Upsolve*) |
| Descriptions | Quote and attribute the publisher's own description and show the source URL and revision date. The AI never writes legal characterizations. | Keeps ShipShape in the publisher and scrivener role |
| Consent | Each suggestion needs an explicit yes or no. Nothing is pre-selected. Remember declines and don't re-suggest the same item unless the user searches for it. | Choice must be real; also avoids nagging dark patterns |
| Source allowlist | Federal .gov forms: mirror and fill, version-checked. State and local forms: link out until copyright terms are checked. CC BY templates: show attribution, license link, version and a "modified/unmodified" note, with no logos. YC SAFE: link only. | 17 U.S.C. 105, *Georgia v. PRO*, CC BY 4.0, YC page terms |
| Template text | Clause text is locked. Users edit field values only. If a license allows changes, the user types them and the AI does not draft, rewrite or "improve" clauses. | *Janson*: turning answers into a legal document |
| Legal-judgment fields | Never autofill governing law, liability caps, IP-assignment scope, tax exemptions or entity choice. Leave them blank with a neutral label such as "You choose." | *Reynoso*: software selecting exemptions |
| Situational questions | Questions like "Should I use an NDA or an MSA?" get a refusal and a link to a lawyer directory or bar referral service. Log every refusal. | DoNotPay order; UPL |
| Blocklist | Wills, trusts and estates; family law; court filings; deeds and anything needing notarization or recording; ESIGN §7003 notice types; employment and litigation documents. | ESIGN exclusions plus the highest UPL risk |
| No human tailoring | Staff never review or customize a user's completed document. | *Janson*'s focus on employee involvement |
| Field detection | Detector-first, LLM-labels pipeline. Show a confidence level for each field, and leave uncertain fields blank and flagged rather than guessed. | FormGym localization failure; Filly's flag-don't-guess approach |
| Claims hygiene | Keep a substantiation file for every number in marketing, such as match rate or fields autofilled. | Workado order |
| Persistent notice | Next to every suggestion: "ShipShape finds and fills documents. It doesn't give legal advice or tell you which document is right for you." | Texas 81.101(c) and NC 84-2.2 models |

Memory re-confirmation should treat every profile fact as a record with five parts: the value, its provenance ("you typed it," "extracted from your uploaded COI on [date]," or "suggested by AI"), the date it was last confirmed, who confirmed it, and its sensitivity class. Facts the AI extracts from uploaded documents enter as *proposed*. They are never used in a fill until the owner confirms them. A counterparty's document never writes into the owner's profile without confirmation either.

Before every fill or passport share, ShipShape shows a review screen listing each fact it is about to use and how old it is, and highlights stale facts for a one-tap confirm or edit. Freshness rules should follow how often each fact type changes. These intervals are a design recommendation, not a sourced standard. Legal name and EIN are re-confirmed yearly or when the entity changes. Addresses, contacts and phone numbers are re-confirmed every few months. COIs and licenses are tracked against their stated expiry dates, with reminders starting 90 days out, which matches YourStanding's practice ([SaaSHub](https://www.saashub.com/yourstanding)).

When a fact changes, ShipShape should list every previously shared W-9, passport link or packet that now carries the old value, and offer to regenerate and re-send it. That turns Gusto's inability to edit a signed W-9 into a ShipShape feature. Sensitive identifiers get stricter handling. ShipShape prefers an EIN over an SSN and lets sole proprietors type an SSN at signing time without storing it. Stored tax IDs stay masked, and revealing one requires step-up authentication and is logged.

Deletion means the fact disappears from every future fill immediately and is purged on the published schedule. That is the direct opposite of Adobe's autofill, whose entries users report keep resurfacing. Memory stays strictly within each business. The only thing shared across users is the field map for each form hash, never values.

Recipients of a passport see the date each document was last confirmed and its expiry. That turns the owner's re-confirmation habit into the very trust signal that collectors currently pay TrustLayer, Jones and BCS to manufacture.

## Conclusion

The research points to one strategic idea. ShipShape's product is not PDF editing, e-signing or even AI filling, all of which are commoditized at $8–$25 a month. Its product is a **dated, owner-confirmed record of business facts**. That record is what a filler needs for accuracy, what a passport recipient needs for trust, and what the privacy promises protect. The re-confirmation loop is a moat, not a compliance chore. It is the feature that turns collector-side tools from competitors into distribution, if ShipShape becomes the source that answers their requests. It is also what separates a "suggest-then-confirm" product from the advice-giving software that courts have punished.

The biggest risks are not technical. One is drift: a product team "improving" suggestions into recommendations, or marketing reaching for "AI lawyer" or "bank-level" language. The other is publishing promises before the controls behind them exist. Both are cheap to prevent now and very expensive later. The FTC and the courts have shown, from DoNotPay to Adobe to Drizly, that they hold companies, and sometimes founders, to their exact words. Several questions remain open and should be settled before launch: whether Docusign's AI consent is on by default, how Anthropic's two retention statements reconcile, whether to register in North Carolina or geofence it, and which JavaScript library will handle PAdES signing.
