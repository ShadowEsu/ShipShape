The problem

Paperwork is a team sport, played over email

Every small business fills out forms. That part is occasional. The part that never ends is sending the same documents to other businesses: a W-9 to every client's accounts-payable team, an insurance certificate to every general contractor, permits to the landlord, an NDA before every meeting. Each requester has its own portal, so the business types the same facts again and again.

The big idea

We don't sell PDF tools. We keep your business's facts true.

PDF editing, e-signing and even AI form filling now sell for $8–25 a month. None of that is a business on its own. What nobody offers is a dated, owner-confirmed record of business facts: your legal name, EIN, address, insurance, licenses, each with where it came from and when you last said "yes, still true."

That one record does three jobs. It makes filling accurate. It gives the people you share with a reason to trust what they receive. And it's the thing our privacy promises protect.

The line we use everywhere

ShipShape remembers your business, checks with you before using anything, and fills, signs and shares your paperwork. It never decides for you.

What we learned

Users complain about billing far more than features. Adobe, Docusign, pdfFiller and Smallpdf all draw the same complaints: trials that turn into annual charges, hard cancellation, and charges after cancelling.

"We don't train AI on your documents" is standard, but most versions have loopholes. Docusign trains on consenting customers' anonymized data, PandaDoc claims rights over AI output data, and Jotform contradicts itself.

Every reusable profile is locked inside someone else's network. Nobody lets a small business own its record and share it anywhere.

Threats to watch
  • LegalZoom's AI license research (Mar 2026) and its Claude connector overlap with "suggesting official forms."
  • A collector network like TrustLayer (517,000+ companies) could add a profile the sender owns. Our answer: become the source that fills their requests, not a rival portal.
  • Rocket Lawyer's $149/yr bundle of unlimited documents and e-sign sets a low price expectation.

How memory works

Every fact is a record with five parts: the value, where it came from ("you typed it", "from your insurance certificate, 2 Sep"), when it was last confirmed, who confirmed it, and how sensitive it is.

The AI only proposes. Facts it reads from your uploads arrive as suggestions and are never used until you tap yes. A document someone else sends you never writes into your profile.

Review before every use. Before any fill or share, you see each fact and its age. Anything stale gets a one-tap check.

Changes ripple out. Change your address and ShipShape lists every W-9 and passport that still shows the old one, then offers to update and re-send them.

Forgetting is real. Delete a fact and it disappears from every future fill at once, and is erased on the published schedule.

The rules behind it

Several results, never one answer. Ranked by how well they match your words.

The publisher speaks, not the AI. We quote the publisher's own description and show the source link and version date.

Nothing is pre-selected. Each result needs your yes. If you say no, we remember and don't suggest it again unless you search for it.

Contract text is locked. You fill in names, dates and amounts. The AI never writes, rewrites or "improves" a clause.

Choices stay yours. Fields that need a legal decision (governing law, liability caps, IP terms) stay blank with "You choose."

We say

  • "Forms matching 'contractor' and 'invoice'"
  • "Businesses searching for this often look at these official forms"
  • "Here's what the publisher says this form is for"
  • "You choose"

We never say

  • "You need this document"
  • "The right document for you"
  • "Based on your situation, we recommend…"
  • "This protects you" / "This makes you compliant"

PDFs done right

Fill it, edit it in the browser, download a clean PDF

The hard part isn't the AI. It's finding exactly where each box sits on a page, which even frontier vision models get almost entirely wrong on their own. So a dedicated detector finds the boxes, and the AI only names them.

FILLABLE PDFS

Like the IRS W-9

We tested the real W-9 (Rev. March 2024). It mixes two form formats and carries Adobe usage rights, so filling takes care:

  1. Load the form and remove the XFA layer and usage-rights entry
  2. Set the tax-class checkboxes by their values (/1–/7) and fill the comb boxes for SSN/EIN
  3. Embed a Unicode font so names like "Zoë Łukasz" work
  4. Regenerate appearances, then flatten for the clean download
  5. Keep an unflattened copy so you can edit again later
FLAT OR SCANNED

Forms with no fields

  1. Render the page as an image
  2. Run FFDNet, an open field detector (81 AP; 93.5 AP on signature boxes)
  3. Snap boxes to the page's own lines
  4. Claude names each numbered box and maps it to a profile fact
  5. Write real form fields into the PDF
  6. Save the field map by the form's fingerprint so the next person filling that official form benefits. We save the map only, never anyone's answers
EDIT IN BROWSER

Your changes, your PDF

  • PDF.js renders pages; a ShipShape layer on top handles fields, text boxes, checkmarks, dates and signatures
  • Each field shows its confidence; uncertain ones stay blank and flagged, never guessed
  • Agreement text is locked; you edit values, add notes, or attach pages
  • Undo, version history, and "compare with original"
NEW DOCUMENTS

Beautiful agreements

Common Paper agreements are typeset with Typst into tagged, accessible PDFs (PDF/UA) with archive-grade versions (PDF/A), with clean typography, page numbers and a cover page.

SIGNING

Three layers

  • Your drawn or typed signature, placed by you
  • A digital seal (PAdES) with a key held in a key vault, so any later change is detectable
  • An audit page: emails, IP addresses, times and document fingerprints before and after signing
  • Single-signer forms in-house; multi-party contracts through BoldSign (about $30/mo + $0.75 per envelope) or Documenso
SAFE UPLOADS

No surprises inside files

  • Uploads go straight from your browser to a quarantine area
  • We check size and page count, reject broken or encrypted files, and strip scripts, auto-actions and embedded files
  • PDF scripting stays off; files are served from a separate domain

COMPLIANCE PATH

Honest about where we are

Hard facts that shape the design
  • The IRS doesn't allow truncating your own TIN on a W-9 you give to others, so the full number must be stored for W-9s, which is why it gets the strongest protection.
  • All 50 states have breach-notification laws, and a name plus SSN triggers every one of them.
  • Supabase advises against its in-database column encryption, and pgsodium is being deprecated, so encryption of secrets happens in our app layer.
  • Vercel functions accept at most 4.5 MB per request, so uploads never pass through them.
Staying out of court

Be a scrivener, never an adviser

Courts protect software that provides blank forms and records the user's own facts. They punish software that applies legal judgment to someone's situation. Every rule below keeps ShipShape on the safe side. A licensed attorney should still review the product before launch.

PRACTICE OF LAW

What the cases say

  • Janson v. LegalZoom (2011): trouble came from a questionnaire plus staff review turning answers into a finished legal document
  • In re Reynoso (2007): software that chose legal options for the user was practicing law
  • Upsolve v. James (2d Cir. Sept 2025; Supreme Court declined Mar 2026): even help with a one-page court form can count
  • Texas protects software that clearly says it's "not a substitute for the advice of an attorney"
  • North Carolina (84-2.2): registration, NC-lawyer review of templates, and no liability limits. Geofence NC or comply
HARD BLOCKLIST

Documents ShipShape will never handle

  • Wills, trusts and estates
  • Family law
  • Court filings and anything for a lawsuit
  • Deeds and anything needing notarization or recording
  • Notices excluded from e-sign law (e.g. foreclosure, insurance cancellation)
  • Employment contracts and offer letters
E-SIGNATURES

Valid when we prove four things

  • Intent to sign, consent to sign electronically, who signed, and a record that can be retrieved later
  • Every signer sees the e-sign consent screen (paper-copy rights, withdrawing consent, requirements)
  • Sealed final PDF plus audit page
BILLING LAW

Design to the strictest rule

  • Federal click-to-cancel rule was struck down (July 2025), but the federal online-subscription law (ROSCA) and state laws still apply
  • California (since July 2025): unchecked consent box, cancel online, annual reminder, keep consent records 3+ years
  • Apply California's standard to everyone
AI CLAIMS

What the FTC still enforces

  • DoNotPay: $193K and a ban on "lawyer substitute" claims it never tested
  • Workado (Aug 2025): no accuracy claims without proof
  • Keep a proof file for every number we publish
TERMS, COMPANY, INSURANCE

The backstop

  • Clickwrap with a clear notice and a clear button (faint gray links lose in court)
  • Liability cap with California and NC carve-outs; arbitration only after weighing mass-arbitration costs
  • LLC or C-corp shields debts, not a founder named personally, so buy tech E&O and cyber insurance and check they don't exclude legal-services claims
Operating rules
  • Staff never review or customize a user's completed document.
  • "Should I use an NDA or an MSA?" gets a polite refusal and a link to a lawyer directory. Every refusal is logged.
  • Every new feature is checked against these rules before it ships. The biggest risk is slowly "improving" suggestions into recommendations.

How the AI works

Five jobs, each with a yes from you

READ

Understand uploads

With your yes, Claude reads the PDF and returns structured data: document type, issuer, dates (effective, expires, notice window) and proposed facts with page numbers and confidence.

PROPOSE

Suggest memory

New or changed facts become suggestions on your confirm screen. Nothing enters your profile without a tap.

LABEL

Map form fields

Claude names detected boxes and matches them to confirmed facts. Low confidence means blank and flagged.

FIND

Search official sources

Searches our curated source list and returns several results with publisher quotes, following the suggestion rules.

ANSWER

Ask your vault

"Where's my NDA with Acme?" Answers come only from your own documents, with page citations. Questions about what you should do get a lawyer referral.

Model: Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5, $4 per million input tokens, $20 per million output), low effort for extraction and medium for questions, U.S. inference, cached system prompts. Deadline checks reuse dates already extracted and never re-send documents. We'll test cheaper models later against a measured test set.

Roadmap

From interviews to a trustworthy launch

WEEKS 1–2 · VALIDATE AND SPIKE

Talk to 25 businesses; prove the hard parts

  • Interviews: how often do you send W-9s, COIs, licenses? Has anything expired unnoticed?
  • Technical spikes: W-9 fill end to end; FFDNet on 20 real flat forms; PAdES sealing in JavaScript; KMS envelope encryption

Go if 10 businesses agree to try it and the W-9 pipeline passes in Acrobat.

WEEKS 3–8 · MVP

Memory, vault, fill, edit, sign, passport

  • Confirm-before-use memory with provenance and hard delete
  • Fill and edit any PDF; sealed download with audit page
  • Passport with per-recipient links, expiry, revoke, view log
  • Security page, subprocessor list, retention schedule, terms reviewed by an attorney

Ship when every promise on the website has its control live.

WEEKS 9–12 · LOOP

Requests, reminders, Collect beta

  • Two-way requests; W-9 shared only on approval
  • Deadline reminders and "update everywhere"
  • Collect beta with 5 GCs or agencies
WEEKS 13–16 · ASSIST

Suggestions and ask-your-vault

  • Curated official-source search with yes/no
  • Vault Q&A with page citations
  • Free public form pages; paid plans on
AFTER

Restaurants, startups, phone app, integrations, SOC 2 Type I

Risks

What could sink it, and the plan for each

Suggestions drift into advice
Written rules, banned phrases, logged refusals, and a review of every new AI feature against the legal section.
Promises published before the controls exist
Each promise has a "live when" gate. Marketing copy ships only with its control.
A data breach
Secrets encrypted per business, row-level security tests, incident plan, cyber insurance, minimal recipient data.
Field detection is wrong on a real form
Flag, don't guess. Shared field maps improve with every user of the same official form.
Recipients want attachments, not links
Every passport exports a single PDF bundle or a plain email with attachments.
A collector network adds owner profiles
Fill their requests from ShipShape instead of competing with their portals.
Decisions

What to decide before we build

North Carolina

Register and comply with 84-2.2, or keep template features off for NC at launch. Recommended: geofence at launch.

Zero data retention with Anthropic

Request it early. Until signed, disclose the provider's 30-day deletion.

Storing SSNs

Recommended: don't store by default; sole proprietors type it at signing, or opt in to encrypted storage.

First customers and state

Recommended: freelancers plus small trade contractors in one state.

Attorney review

Before launch: terms, privacy policy, suggestion rules, NC plan, e-sign consent text.

Assistant name

"First Mate" or simply "Ask ShipShape".
