01 · The problem

Paperwork is a team sport, played over email

Every small business fills out forms. That part is annoying but rare. The part that never ends is sending the same documents to other businesses: a W-9 to every client's accounts-payable team, an insurance certificate to every general contractor, permits to the landlord, an NDA before every meeting.

Today that happens through attachments, "can you resend the updated one?" and documents that expire without anyone noticing. Nobody owns the problem, because every tool on the market is built for one person filling one form alone.

"Every new client asks for my W-9 and sends me their NDA. I dig through Downloads every time."
  • Sends a W-9 and signs an NDA for every new client
  • Re-types her address and EIN into every form
  • January: five clients ask for W-9s for 1099s at once

  • Insurance renewal means re-sending to everyone
  • Works from his phone, between jobs

  • Sends permits and COIs to landlord, delivery apps, caterers
  • Collects W-9s and certificates from her own vendors
  • Renewal dates scattered across agencies

Sam, first-time founder

  • Sends SAFEs to investors, NDAs to partners
  • Customers' procurement asks for W-9 and security docs
  • Needs the same company details on every form

04 · The product

Four pieces, one passport

ShipShape is your business's paperwork passport: everything other businesses ask you for, always current, shared with one link.

MEMORY

It remembers your business

  • Legal name, DBA, EIN, address, entity type, state, license and policy numbers, signature
  • Learns from every document you upload or fill
  • Every fact shows where it came from ("from your W-9, Jan 2026")
  • Never overwrites silently. It asks: "Your address changed. Update everywhere?"
VAULT

Every document, findable

  • Upload PDFs or phone photos; ShipShape names and files them
  • Fill any PDF form from your memory in seconds
  • Tap to place your signature. It's never applied automatically
  • Full history: source, version, what was filled, who signed
PASSPORT

One link that's always current

  • Share a packet: W-9, COI, license, NDA to sign
  • Choose what each recipient sees; set expiry; see who viewed
  • When a document renews, every recipient gets the new one
  • Requests run both ways: ask a vendor for their W-9 and COI, and they fill it through ShipShape
DEADLINES

Nothing lapses quietly

  • Reads renewal, expiry and notice dates out of your documents
  • Reminds you 60, 30 and 7 days ahead
  • Flags what your recipients are waiting on
  • Shows a single "shipshape" status: everything current, or what isn't
The AI assistant · working name "First Mate"

"Where's my NDA with Acme?" · "What did I agree to pay the designer?" · "What expires this quarter?" · "Send my COI to the new GC."

07 · How the AI works

AI that reads and fills, and never writes the law

The AI reads your documents, remembers facts, fills fields and finds things. It never drafts or changes contract terms and never tells anyone what they should sign.

STAGE 1

Read

Each upload (PDF or photo) goes to Claude, which reads PDFs natively. A structured-output schema returns:

  • doc_type, issuer, parties
  • dates: effective, expires, renewal notice window
  • facts: key, value, page, confidence
  • a one-line plain-English summary
STAGE 2

Remember

Extracted facts are compared with the profile. New facts become suggestions; changed facts ask before updating. Each fact keeps its source document, page and date, so the app can always say where it learned something.

STAGE 3

Fill

  • Fillable PDFs: read field names with pdf-lib; the AI maps each field to a profile key with a confidence score
  • Flat or scanned PDFs: render pages to images; the AI locates labels and boxes; values are drawn as an overlay
  • Low-confidence fields are highlighted for the user. Fields with no matching fact stay blank; nothing is invented
STAGE 4

Watch

Dates feed a deadlines table that drives reminders and the passport status. A nightly re-check runs through the Message Batches API, which costs half as much as live calls.

STAGE 5

Answer

Questions search only the user's own documents (Postgres full-text plus vector search, isolated by row-level security). The AI answers with page citations, so every answer links to the source.

Guardrails built into the system prompt and the code
  • Explains what a document says, never what the user should do. "Should I sign this?" leads to "Talk to a lawyer"
  • Never generates or edits clauses; the original document text is locked
  • SSNs, EINs and bank numbers are detected and swapped for tokens before any AI call, then filled back in on our servers
  • Documents are never used to train models; each business's data is isolated

Model and cost

We start with Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5, $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output) for every stage. Effort runs low for extraction and medium for questions. The fixed system prompt and schemas are cached, and nightly jobs run in batch. Our rough estimate is a few cents per document processed. Once we have a measured test set, we can test cheaper models for high-volume steps.

09 · Design

Calm, clear, and easy for anyone to use

The bar: Luis can share his passport from a truck in two minutes, and a founder thinks it looks like it belongs next to Stripe.

Show, don't ask

Prefill everything we know, with a small source chip. Ask only for what's missing.

One decision per screen

Plain words: "Insurance certificate", not "ACORD 25". No legal jargon in the interface.

Status at a glance

Green: current. Amber: renewing soon. Red: expired. Shown the same way everywhere.

Phone first

Camera upload, thumb-reachable actions, passport pages that load fast on bad signal.

Make trust visible

Who viewed what and when, link expiry, lock icons on sensitive fields, one-tap revoke.

A professional handoff

The recipient's view is the brand's billboard: clean, fast, "Powered by ShipShape".

Brand idea. "Shipshape" means everything in good order. The identity borrows from passports and harbor charts: deep harbor blue, passport-stamp marks for status, and a machine-readable line as a signature detail. The tone is confident and quiet, like a good accountant.

10 · Trust & legal

Designed so there's little to sue over

No terms of service make a company impossible to sue. Our protection is a product that stays clearly on the software side of the line. The full analysis is in docs/legal-risk-playbook.md.

Never gives legal advice or picks terms for you
Never writes or edits contract clauses
Never signs for you automatically
Never re-hosts documents we lack rights to
Never trains AI on your documents
Never markets itself as a lawyer
RiskHow we handle it
Unauthorized practice of lawSoftware only: search, fill facts, store, share. A "talk to a lawyer" option on every document. North Carolina's document-site rules met before serving NC (G.S. 84-2.2).
A user's document fails themOfficial, attorney-drafted sources only. A required review screen with a logged acknowledgment before signing or downloading. Full audit trail.
CopyrightCC BY sources (Common Paper) with attribution, public government forms, user uploads. YC SAFE through a link-out until permission is granted.
FTC / advertisingNo "AI lawyer" or "guaranteed binding" claims. DoNotPay paid $193K over exactly that.
Data breachEncryption, RLS, tokenized sensitive numbers, short-lived links, revocation, audit log. SOC 2 once we sell to larger buyers.
DisputesAttorney-drafted terms: arbitration, class-action waiver, liability capped at fees paid. Delaware C-corp or LLC, plus tech E&O and cyber insurance.

15 · Risks

What could sink it, and the plan for each

Recipients want attachments, not links
Every passport also exports a single PDF bundle or zip, and can send a plain email with attachments. The link is a convenience, not a gate.
People won't trust us with sensitive documents
Visible security: who viewed, expiry, revoke, tokenized numbers. Security page, then SOC 2. Start with documents that are already semi-public (W-9s, COIs, licenses).
An incumbent adds sharing
Speed and the network. Every passport and request we deliver makes switching harder. Incumbents sell documents; we run exchanges.
Fake or edited certificates
We show "uploaded by the business" and never claim verification. Later: direct feeds from insurance brokers and state license lookups for a "Verified" badge.
Scope creep into legal advice
The "never" list is a product rule. New features get a quick check against it, and the attorney reviews flows before launch.
Too broad to explain
One message everywhere: "Your business paperwork, filled and shared with one link." Segments only change the starter kit.
16 · Decisions

First wedge

Recommended: freelancers plus small trade contractors. They have the most repeat sending and the clearest growth loop.

Launch state

Assistant name

"First Mate" fits the brand. Keep it, or simply "Ask ShipShape".

Legal budget

A few hours with a legal-tech attorney before public launch: product flows, terms, privacy policy.
