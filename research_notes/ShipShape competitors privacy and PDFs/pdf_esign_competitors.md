# PDF Editing, Form-Filling and E-Signature Competitors to ShipShape (as of Oct 2026, US focus)

Scope: pdfFiller / airSlate (signNow, US Legal Forms, DocHub), Adobe Acrobat (Fill & Sign, AI Assistant, Acrobat Studio / Express), Docusign (eSignature, IAM, Iris AI), Dropbox Sign (ex-HelloSign), PandaDoc, Jotform Sign, Smallpdf.

Source-quality note: Most vendors' official pricing pages could not be fetched directly in this pass. Many price points come from third-party pricing trackers, and some of those are run by competitors (eversign, goodsign, verdocs, SignWell, esign.ai, and PandaDoc writing about Docusign) that have an interest in making rivals look expensive. Conflicts are flagged inline. Treat every price as "verify on vendor page before publishing."

## 1. How does each make money and what do they charge small businesses?

### Takeaway
All seven run on recurring SaaS subscriptions. They push annual billing, which typically halves the "headline" monthly price versus month-to-month, and they gate features by tier and by caps on sends, envelopes or signatures. For a solo or small business, typical 2026 entry prices fall between about $8 and $25 per user per month billed annually. Team or "business" tiers ($25–$65 per user per month) are where bulk send, templates, branding and integrations sit. Docusign now leads its pricing page with its pricier IAM plans.

### Cited Findings

**pdfFiller / airSlate**
- airSlate runs a portfolio of about 27 products, including pdfFiller, signNow, DocHub and US Legal Forms — [bestguide.com (aggregator)](https://bestguide.com/review/pdffiller/)
- pdfFiller's own "Facts for AI" page lists Basic at $8/mo, Plus at $12/mo and Premium at $15/mo, all billed annually. US Legal Forms template access is included "only on the Premium plan." It advertises a "30-day free trial" with "no credit card required to start." — [pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)
- Monthly-billed prices are much higher: Basic $20/user/mo (vs $8 annual) and Premium $40/mo for up to 3 users (vs $15 annual) — [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/reviews/162654/pdffiller); [Tekpon](https://tekpon.com/software/pdffiller/pricing/) lists $20/$30/$40 monthly. The Capterra IE/UK listing of Plus at $50/user/mo annual conflicts with the other sources and is probably an error. A May 2026 xpay snapshot showed all tiers as "Contact sales" — [xpay.sh](https://www.xpay.sh/agent-ready-index/pdffiller/). Public pricing may be shifting.
- Plus adds reusable templates, basic fillable fields and version restore. Premium adds signNow signing and the US Legal Forms library — [Capterra pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/162654/PDFfiller/pricing/)

**Adobe Acrobat**
- 2026 US individual pricing (reported as of July 30, 2026), on annual contracts billed monthly: Standard $14.99/mo, Pro $19.99/mo, Studio $24.99/mo. Month-to-month: $24.99, $29.99 and $34.99. Teams: Standard $16.99, Pro $23.99, Studio $29.99 per license per month — [Redress Compliance](https://redresscompliance.com/adobe-acrobat-pricing-ai-features-2026); [Photutorial](https://photutorial.com/adobe-acrobat-pricing/) (third-party)
- The AI Assistant add-on for Standard and Pro is most often cited at about $4.99/mo annual or $7.99/mo monthly. Sources range from $1.99 up to an implausible "~$30". Studio includes AI Assistant — [Redress Compliance](https://redresscompliance.com/adobe-acrobat-pricing-ai-features-2026); [aisotools](https://aisotools.com/blog/adobe-acrobat-ai-review-2026)
- Acrobat Studio launched in August 2025 at $24.99/mo for individuals and $29.99/mo for teams (early-access pricing) with a 14-day free trial. It bundles Acrobat Pro, Adobe Express Premium, AI Assistants and "PDF Spaces" — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/adobe-acrobat-studio/); [IT Brief US](https://itbrief.news/story/adobe-launches-acrobat-studio-with-ai-pdf-tools)

**Docusign**
- August 2026 prices (third-party, written by competitor PandaDoc): Personal $10/mo annual or $15/mo monthly. Standard $25/user/mo annual or $45 monthly. Business Pro $40/user/mo annual or $65 monthly. Professional Unlimited $60/user/mo, annual only. Personal is capped at 5 envelopes. Standard and Business Pro are capped at 100 envelopes per user per year. The pricing page now leads with IAM plans "from $65 per user", and the cheaper eSignature plans sit on a second tab. - IAM originally launched to SMB and mid-market customers, then expanded to enterprises — [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/node/38725)

**Dropbox Sign**
- Free: $0 for 3 signature requests per month. Essentials: $15/mo or $180/yr, single sender, 5 templates. Standard: $25/user/mo with a 2-user minimum ($600/yr), 15 templates, branding, bulk send and integrations. Premium: custom quote (SSO, SMS auth, data residency) — [eversign guide](https://eversign.com/blog/dropbox-sign-pricing-guide); [eversign Essentials vs Standard](https://eversign.com/blog/dropbox-sign-essentials-vs-standard-plans); [goodsign](https://goodsign.io/dropbox-sign-pricing-hacks) (competitor-run sites). An outlier lists Essentials at $20 and Standard at $30/user with a 3-user minimum — [subger](https://subger.com/en/service/hellosign) (likely outdated)
- API plans are priced separately. Embedded signing reportedly requires Standard API or higher, at about $300/mo — [toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/dropbox/pricing) (secondary, unverified)

**PandaDoc**
- Starter is $19/seat/mo annual or $35 monthly. Business is $49/seat/mo annual or $65 monthly. Enterprise is custom. Paid plans have a 14-day trial. Free eSign plan reportedly limited to 60 docs/yr — [bindlegal](https://bindlegal.com/resources/comparisons/pandadoc-pricing-2026/); [Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/pandadoc-pricing); [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/document-management/pandadoc-docs/)
- API, web forms, bulk send, notarization and QES are commonly paid add-ons. A Starter document cap (110 docs/yr) was reportedly published in April–July 2026 and then withdrawn — sources conflict — [goodsign](https://goodsign.io/pandadoc-pricing-hacks); [costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/contract-management/pandadoc/)
- Business plans are billed for the full year upfront, and seat counts are locked for that period (G2 reviewer) — [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/pandadoc/worth-it)

**Jotform Sign**
- Sign is bundled into Jotform plans. Monthly prices: Bronze $39, Silver $49, Gold $129. Annual prices conflict between sources ($34/$39/$99 per one guide; Sign-specific $19.50/$24.50/$64.50 per SignWell, a competitor) — [Zite](https://www.zite.com/blog/jotform-pricing); [SignWell](https://www.signwell.com/resources/?p=4463)
- Signed-document caps per month: Starter (free) 10, Bronze 100, Silver 250, Gold 1,000. These cannot be extended on the free plan — [Jotform Answers](https://www.jotform.com/answers/40282401-signature-limit-on-starter-plan); [Jotform Answers](https://www.jotform.com/answers/41337151-extension-of-signatures)
- No overage billing. Hitting a cap disables forms ("Form over quota") until the cap resets or you upgrade — [usagepricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/jotform)

**Smallpdf**
- Free, Pro, Team (2–19 seats) and sales-quoted Business tiers. Pro includes unlimited AI tools — [Smallpdf pricing](https://smallpdf.com/pricing); [Smallpdf Teams](https://smallpdf.com/teams)
- Pro is about $15/mo and Pro for Teams about $12/user/mo (verified March 2026), with team pricing from about $8/user/mo annual — [TemperStack](https://www.temperstack.com/plans/smallpdf/); [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/172606/Smallpdf/pricing/). Users report a $108 yearly charge after the trial — [sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/smallpdf-us/i-face-108-charge-from-smallpdf-after-free-trial-ended-without-notice)

### Inferences
- The "real" monthly price is often roughly 2x the advertised one (pdfFiller $8 vs $20, Docusign $25 vs $45, PandaDoc $19 vs $35). That gap is itself a common source of sticker-shock complaints. A newcomer can win trust by showing one price, or by showing monthly and annual side by side.
- Caps (Docusign envelopes, Jotform signed docs, Dropbox Sign templates and requests) are the main upsell lever. That hits exactly the ShipShape persona who sends the same packet to many counterparties.
- Docusign is moving upmarket to IAM and burying cheap plans. pdfFiller may be hiding public pricing ("Contact sales" snapshot). Both open room for a transparent SMB product.

### Gaps
- Official vendor pricing pages for Docusign, Dropbox Sign, PandaDoc, Jotform and Smallpdf were not fetched directly. Figures are third-party.
- Revenue mix (subscription vs API vs enterprise) and airSlate's private financials were not researched.

## 2. What do their AI features actually do, and what do they say about training on customer documents?

### Takeaway
AI features cluster around "chat with / summarize a PDF", AI form filling and field detection, AI document generation, and (for Docusign and PandaDoc) contract-term extraction and agents. Most vendors now say they don't train on customer documents. The exceptions are wording nuances:
- **Docusign:** trains on consenting customers' aggregated and anonymized data, and how consent is set by default is unclear.
- **PandaDoc:** its AI Addendum grants rights to "AI Improvement Data" that are broader than its help-center promise.
- **Jotform:** contradicts itself across its own policies.
- **pdfFiller/airSlate:** no AI training statement found at all.
- **Adobe:** carries reputational baggage from the June 2024 terms-of-use backlash.

### Cited Findings

**pdfFiller / airSlate**
- AI features: "AI PDF Editor for natural-language editing", "Chat with PDF", AI Summarizer, Explainer, Proofreader, Translator, "AI Form Filler and AI Document Creator" — [pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)
- The same page makes no statement on whether customer data trains AI. Security claims: 256-bit AES, SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA/GDPR/CCPA/PCI DSS "for relevant use cases and plans" — [pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)
- The only airSlate privacy policy found is a 2020 PDF hosted by a third party, with no AI terms — [DLT-hosted airSlate Privacy Policy (2020)](https://www.dlt.com/sites/default/files/documents/2020-10/AirSlate-Privacy-Policy.pdf)
- Its sibling signNow markets "AI-driven tools to fill PDF forms" — [signNow](https://www.signnow.com/esignature/ai-to-fill-pdf-forms)

**Adobe Acrobat**
- Acrobat AI Assistant summarizes, answers questions and cites sources. The enterprise version (launched May 2024) has admin controls. "No customer document content is used to train the LLM Acrobat AI Assistant leverages." — [Adobe press release, May 2024](https://news.adobe.com/news/news-details/2024/adobe-revolutionizes-workplace-productivity-with-launch-of-acrobat-ai-assistant-for-the-enterprise)
- Acrobat's generative AI uses Microsoft Azure OpenAI Service, which is "contractually prohibited from manually reviewing or training its LLM on Adobe customer data" — [Adobe business blog](https://business.adobe.com/blog/adobe-acrobat-ai-assistant-enterprise-our-commitment-data-governance-security). Adobe's current business page says Acrobat "does not use customer content to train generative AI models" — [Adobe Acrobat business](https://business.adobe.com/in/products/acrobat-business.html)
- Acrobat Studio (Aug 2025) adds "PDF Spaces", which turn collections of up to 100+ PDFs and web pages into AI-driven interactive hubs, plus Firefly-powered Express content creation — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/adobe-acrobat-studio/); [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/inteligencia-artificial/novo-recurso-de-ia-da-adobe-organiza-mais-de-100-pdfs-de-uma-vez-conheca/)
- Fill & Sign "autofill collection" uses Adobe Sensei AI to "detect the right field and automatically fill it out next time" — [DirectIndustry (Adobe product listing)](https://directindustry.com/prod/adobe/product-31646-2537015.html)
- **The 2024 terms-of-service controversy:** the General Terms were changed on Feb 17, 2024. In June 2024, a forced "re-acceptance" pop-up for Creative Cloud and Document Cloud users triggered backlash over broad license language. On June 10, Adobe stated "We don't train generative AI on customer content" and said it was adding this as a legal commitment. CPO Scott Belsky called the wording "unclear." Revised terms around June 18, 2024 stated that Adobe does not scan locally stored content and does not train gen-AI on user content except Adobe Stock submissions, and added a product-improvement opt-out — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2024/06/no-ai-training-in-newly-distrusted-terms-of-service-adobe-says); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/06/adobe-updates-its-terms-of-use-following-artist-backlash); [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/adobe-ai-training-backlash). Commentators argued some concerns remain — [Substack commentary](https://kbssidhu.substack.com/p/adobes-controversial-terms-of-service)

**Docusign**
- January 2026: eSignature was rebuilt on Docusign Iris (its "agreement AI engine") with AI document preparation and an AI-assisted signing experience — [Docusign Jan 2026 release notes](https://docusign.com/releases/january-2026)
- May 21, 2026: an AI assistant and agents were announced, along with CRM-embedded Agreement Desk, Agreement Prep and Agreement Manager. Iris surfaces key terms, obligations and renewal dates. Purpose-built contract agents that flag risks were slated for "later this year" — [Docusign news](https://www.docusign.com/company/news-center/docusign-unveils-ai-assistant-and-agents-to-power-the-next-era-of-agreement-work); [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/index%2ephp/insights/news/docusign-launches-ai-contract-agents)
- Docusign's AI trust page: "Your data are only used for AI/ML training with your consent, and you have the flexibility to manage your consent." "If you consent… everything is aggregated and anonymised before use." The page does not state whether the default is opt-in or opt-out — [Docusign AI Trust](https://www.docusign.com/en-gb/products/platform/ai/ai-trust)
- Customer account admins control this through an "AI Data Controls" in-product setting — [Docusign blog (CPO)](https://www.docusign.com/en-sg/blog/ai-powered-future-customer-control); [Docusign community FAQ](https://community.docusign.com/docusign-partners-133/faq-how-do-i-turn-off-use-of-my-account-data-for-ai-training-24940)
- February/March 2024 controversy: critics said Docusign's statement that customers "contractually consent" to AI training use was opaque, and noted that anonymization can be reversed — [jmason.ie, Mar 2024](https://jmason.ie/2024/03/01/175902a.html); [AlternativeTo, Mar 2024](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/3/docusign-to-use-customer-data-for-ai-training-raising-privacy-concerns-among-users). A terms tracker reads the AI Attachment as consent with opt-out — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/docusign/docusign-terms-and-conditions/ai-training-data-use-and-opt-out/) (not a legal determination). **Conflict:** Docusign's own pages frame this as consent-based. Third parties frame it as default-on with opt-out.

**Dropbox Sign**
- No Sign-specific AI feature or AI-training statement was found in this pass.
- Parent Dropbox's December 2023 controversy: a "third-party AI" toggle was on by default for Dropbox AI alpha users. CEO Drew Houston said no data is sent "automatically or passively." Dropbox said third-party data is deleted within 30 days and not used for training. The toggle was later hidden — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/12/36298097/dropbox-ceo-denies-sharing-user-data-with-openai-but-the-option-to-disable-ai-search-is-now-hidden); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2023/Dec/14/ai-trust-crisis/); [Computing](https://computing.co.uk/news/4157118/dropbox-backlash-openai-sharing)

**PandaDoc**
- 2026 AI: AI Document Assistant (a chatbot for search and contract summaries, plus auto-organizing), AI data extraction, AI email suggestions, and a native MCP server that lets external AI assistants act on documents — [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/pandadoc/worth-it) (aggregator)
- OpenAI, LLC is a listed subprocessor for LLM processing, with US hosting — [PandaDoc subprocessors](https://www.pandadoc.com/legal/subprocessors/)
- Help center: "Customer Content is not used to train any AI LLMs." Third-party AI providers are prohibited from training on or retaining data. The related AI data toggle is off by default. AI is disabled for EU-hosted accounts — [PandaDoc AI policy](https://support.pandadoc.com/en/articles/13794735-pandadoc-ai-policy); [PandaDoc AI data privacy settings](https://support.pandadoc.com/en/articles/16106373-ai-data-privacy-settings)
- **Conflict:** the AI Addendum defines "AI Training" as using "AI Improvement Data" (derived from AI Output) to train PandaDoc's AI/ML models, with a perpetual license and PandaDoc ownership. Customers may opt out of AI Services going forward — [PandaDoc AI Addendum](https://www.pandadoc.com/legal/ai-addendum/)

**Jotform Sign**
- Jotform AI Agents mainly run on OpenAI models. Form content personalizes agents and is not used to train Jotform models. OpenAI keeps API data for 30 days for abuse monitoring (other language versions cite Zero Data Retention). Jotform has BAAs with OpenAI and Google — [Jotform AI Agents security](https://eu.jotform.com/ai-agents/security)
- **Internal inconsistency:** Jotform's AI Policy says personal data is never used to train its AI. It also says data in form fields "may be used to train" its AI. The AI Terms say Jotform won't train on your input but make no promise about providers — [Jotform AI Policy](https://www.jotform.com/ai-policy/); [Jotform AI Terms](https://jotform.com/ai-terms)
- Sign auto-detects fillable fields in uploaded PDFs — [Jotform Sign features](https://www.jotform.com/products/sign/features)

**Smallpdf**
- Its AI Products Supplementary Terms say Smallpdf does not use customer data, or allow others to use it, to train the models behind its AI products. Usage data is kept internally and not shared with OpenAI for training — [Smallpdf AI supplementary terms](https://smallpdf.com/ai-products-supplementary-terms)
- Claims ISO/IEC 27001, GDPR, CCPA and nFADP compliance, and says files are deleted after processing — [Smallpdf Teams](https://smallpdf.com/teams); [G2](https://g2.com/products/smallpdf/reviews/smallpdf-review-9479095)

### Inferences
- "We don't train on your documents" is now table stakes. Adobe, Smallpdf and PandaDoc's help center all say it. To stand out, ShipShape needs a stronger, contractual, plain-English promise:
  - no training by us or any subprocessor
  - no "anonymized aggregate" carve-out
  - no "AI output/improvement data" license
  - an off-by-default posture
  - a published subprocessor list

  That directly contrasts with Docusign's consent-to-train model, PandaDoc's AI Addendum and Jotform's contradictory policy.
- None of the AI features found are aimed at "know my business and fill every form with confirmed facts." They are document-centric (summarize or edit this PDF) rather than business-profile-centric.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether Docusign's AI-training consent defaults on or off for SMB eSignature accounts. The AI Attachment was not read directly.
- No current airSlate/pdfFiller AI-training or AI-retention statement was found. This is a notable silence; recommend checking pdffiller.com/privacy-policy directly.
- No Dropbox Sign-specific AI disclosures were found.
- Adobe AI Assistant upload retention: one search snippet mentioned 12-hour deletion, but this was not verified.

## 3. What are the most common user complaints (billing traps, hard cancellation, free-trial bait, privacy)?

### Takeaway
Billing and cancellation dominate complaints across the category:
- **Adobe:** the only federal enforcement action found, a $150M DOJ/FTC ROSCA settlement in March 2026.
- **pdfFiller/airSlate:** a BBB "B" rating driven by about 390 complaints with an "unauthorized charges" pattern, and free trials that turn into annual charges.
- **Smallpdf:** trial-to-annual charges.
- **Docusign:** renewals charged after cancellation, and paywalled support.
- **PandaDoc:** annual-upfront seat lock-in.
- **Dropbox Sign:** the headline privacy incident, a May 2024 production breach.

### Cited Findings

**Adobe (federal action)**
- The DOJ (with the FTC) filed a proposed stipulated order on March 13, 2026. Adobe pays a $75M civil penalty plus $75M in free services to customers ($150M total), resolving ROSCA claims against Adobe and executives Maninder Sawhney and David Wadhwani.
  - The government alleged Adobe used "fine print and inconspicuous hyperlinks" to hide a "hefty Early Termination Fee." It also alleged Adobe "thwarted subscribers' attempts to cancel" with "convoluted and inefficient cancellation processes filled with unnecessary steps, delays, unsolicited offers, and warnings."
  - The order requires Adobe to disclose the ETF and how it is calculated before enrollment, to send reminders before converting free trials longer than 7 days into ETF-bearing subscriptions, and to provide easy cancellation.
  - Quote from US Attorney Craig Missakian: "Consumers should not have to navigate a digital maze to cancel a subscription."

  — [DOJ press release 26-252, Mar 13 2026](https://business.cch.com/ald/OfficeofPublicAffairs_AdobeAgreesto150MillionSettlementandInjunctiontoResolveAllegedViolationsoftheRestoreOnlineShoppersConfidenceAct_UnitedStatesDepartmentofJustice032026.pdf)
- Court approval was reportedly granted April 10, 2026 by Judge Noel Wise, without admission of wrongdoing — [cancelsubscriptionsapp explainer](https://cancelsubscriptionsapp.com/guides/adobe-early-termination-fee-explained) (secondary; verify on the docket)
- The original FTC/DOJ complaint (June 2024) quoted an internal description of the ETF as being "like heroin" for Adobe's business — [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/money/fees/ftc-lawsuit-alleges-adobe-fails-to-tell-subscribers-about-early-termination-fee/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-sues-adobe-and-execs-illegal-hidden-fees)
- Fill & Sign autofill privacy complaints: users report saved entries persisting after disabling Auto-Complete or reinstalling, and cached data reappearing (forum posts from 2020–2024) — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-discussions/turn-off-all-autocomplete-and-autosuggestions-in-fill-amp-sign-mode/m-p/14413162); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-discussions/clear-history-of-fill-amp-sign-autocomplete-forms/td-p/11277239)

**pdfFiller / airSlate**
- airSlate, Inc. has been BBB-accredited since 2013 and has a B rating citing 390 complaints. In April 2024, a BBB AI analysis reportedly flagged a pattern of "unauthorized charges," often via linked Venmo accounts ($20–$180) — [bestguide.com (aggregator; verify on bbb.org)](https://bestguide.com/review/pdffiller/)
- Trustpilot themes: charges continuing after cancellation, a full annual subscription charged despite cancelling the trial, and a cancel flow leading to a profile "not clearly linked" to the account. Other reviewers report quick refunds — [Trustpilot (AU)](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/pdffiller.com)
- A 2023 Deceptive Design tip-line report says the pdfFiller Android app lets you complete a document and then requires a subscription to send or save it, and that the "free trial" still asks for a card (unverified) — [Deceptive Design](https://deceptive.design/articles/airslate-inc-pdffiller-requires-subscription-after-document-completion-and-free-trial-still-asks-for-cc-info)
- Payments are described as non-refundable "regardless if you used the products or not," with disputes accepted within 30 days of an invoice (third-party summary of the terms) — [ComplaintsBoard](https://www.complaintsboard.com/pdffiller-b124811/contacts)
- **Conflict:** pdfFiller's own page now says "no credit card required to start" the trial — [pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)

**Docusign**
- Users report renewal charges after cancelling: $591.78 taken right after a downgrade to free, and about $1,000 a month after cancelling. Moderators say billing can't be discussed publicly and redirect users to support cases — [Docusign Community 23641](https://community.docusign.com/esignature-111/i-was-charged-even-after-canceling-subscription-23641/index3.html); [Docusign Community 22179](https://community.docusign.com/esignature-111/charged-even-after-canceling-subscription-22179/index2.html); [Docusign Community 10340](https://community.docusign.com/questions/10340)

- Trustpilot reportedly about 1.4/5, with themes of billing, cancellation and support (not verified on Trustpilot directly) — [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/document-management/electronic-signature-software/docusign)

**Dropbox Sign**

  - Names and emails of non-account signers were also exposed.
  - No document contents or payment information were accessed, per Dropbox.

  — [Dropbox 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762324000024/may2024exhibit991.htm); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2024/05/dropbox-sign-customer-data-accessed-in-breach); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2024/05/02/dropbox-sign-breached/)

**PandaDoc**
- Annual upfront billing with seat counts locked for the year; complaints that it is expensive for small teams; slow lower-tier support; billing issues taking multiple contacts; consumer review score about 3.0/5 (638 reviews) — [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/pandadoc/worth-it); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/pandadoc/details)

**Regulatory context**
- The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's "Click-to-Cancel" rule in full on July 8, 2025 (Custom Communications v. FTC) on procedural grounds. ROSCA and state automatic-renewal laws still apply — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/node/15872)

### Inferences

The Adobe settlement gives a concrete, citable federal benchmark (ETF disclosure, trial reminders, easy cancel). ShipShape can promise to exceed it voluntarily.
- With the federal click-to-cancel rule vacated, ROSCA plus state laws are the floor. Voluntary one-click cancel and pre-renewal reminders are a real differentiator, not just compliance.
- Signers' names and emails were exposed in the Dropbox Sign breach even though the signers had no account. ShipShape's "passport" sharing model means counterparties' data will also be held, so security messaging has to cover non-users too.

- FTC follow-up on click-to-cancel rulemaking in 2026 is unknown.

## 4. Do any offer a persistent business profile, reusable document packets, or two-way document requests?

### Takeaway
None of the seven offers a true persistent business profile that is confirmed with the user and auto-fills arbitrary official forms. None offers a shareable live "paperwork passport." Closest analogs:
- **Adobe Fill & Sign:** an autocomplete-style "autofill collection" of past entries.
- **pdfFiller:** a 1M+ form library plus LinkToFill share links.
- **Docusign:** Web Forms with prefill from a system of record and attachment fields for signer uploads.
- **Jotform / Dropbox Sign / PandaDoc:** templates and reusable send-many documents.

Two-way requests exist only in narrow forms: signer attachment fields, web-form collection, and bulk send.

### Cited Findings
- **Adobe:** Fill & Sign keeps a "personal autofill collection" and AI fills matching fields next time — [DirectIndustry (Adobe listing)](https://directindustry.com/prod/adobe/product-31646-2537015.html).
  - It is built on Auto-Complete, which stores "any entries that you type in an interactive form field" — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/delete-auto-fill-data-in-acrobat/m-p/14553250).
  - It is history-based: Acrobat "builds new suggestions based on the forms you fill out" — [Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/lt/acrobat/mobile/preferences/clear-form-data.html).
  - There is no structured, verified profile, and no re-confirmation step.
- **pdfFiller:**
  - "Library of 1M+ fillable forms," including IRS and government forms
  - "Convert static PDFs into reusable fillable forms and templates," with shared team templates
  - LinkToFill: "Publish forms via shareable links… and collect responses"
  - an "AI Form Filler"

  — [pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)
- **US Legal Forms** (airSlate) provides legal templates, but only on pdfFiller Premium — [pdfFiller Facts for AI](https://www.pdffiller.com/en/ai-instructions.htm)
- **Docusign:**
  - The Web Forms API and JS library can prefill form fields from a system of record — [Docusign developer blog](https://docusign.com/blog/developers/web-forms-api-and-embedded-forms).
  - Signers can upload requested documents through attachment fields in templates. Fields with identical data labels prompt once and populate everywhere (community moderator answer) — [Docusign Community 25027](https://community.docusign.com/esignature-111/docusign-as-pre-start-vehicle-25027).
  - Supplemental documents can be added to envelopes — [Docusign features](https://www.docusign.com/en-us/products/electronic-signature/features).
  - IAM/Iris extracts terms, obligations and renewal dates from executed agreements (a repository, not a business profile) — [Docusign news May 2026](https://www.docusign.com/company/news-center/docusign-unveils-ai-assistant-and-agents-to-power-the-next-era-of-agreement-work)
- **Jotform Sign:**
  - "Create your sign document once and send it multiple times to multiple people"
  - 800+ templates
  - public/private links and embeds
  - approval flows, reminders and signer delegation

  — [Jotform Sign features](https://www.jotform.com/products/sign/features). Saving an uploaded PDF as a reusable template is reportedly awkward (unresolved support thread) — [Jotform Answers](https://www.jotform.com/answers/41632591-jotform-sign-request-to-create-and-save-custom-templates-for-pdf-uploads)
- **Dropbox Sign:** templates are capped at 5 (Essentials) or 15 (Standard), and bulk send requires Standard — [eversign](https://eversign.com/blog/dropbox-sign-essentials-vs-standard-plans)
- **PandaDoc:** web forms and bulk send are add-ons, and its AI auto-organizes files — [goodsign](https://goodsign.io/pandadoc-pricing-hacks); [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/pandadoc/worth-it)
- **Smallpdf:** no profile or packet features were identified. It is positioned as a per-file tool suite with AI — [Smallpdf pricing](https://smallpdf.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- The "remember my business, re-confirm, then fill any official form" loop is unclaimed. Adobe's version is passive autocomplete and generated privacy complaints about data persisting. ShipShape can position its profile as explicit, editable, re-confirmed and deletable, the opposite of Adobe's sticky autofill.
- Reusable "packets" (e.g., W-9 + COI + license + NDA sent to every new client or vendor) are approximated only by templates and bulk send. Those are tier-gated and capped, and they are built around one document needing a signature, not a standing bundle of business credentials.
- Two-way requests (counterparty asks for your docs, and you ask for theirs) aren't productized anywhere in this set. The nearest are Docusign attachment fields and pdfFiller LinkToFill/Jotform forms, which are one-directional collection.
- pdfFiller's 1M+ form library is the closest competitor to "suggest official forms." ShipShape's differentiators are proactive suggestion based on the profile plus explicit yes/no consent.

### Gaps
- Dropbox's core product has "File Requests" (upload-only links). It was not researched or confirmed as integrated with Dropbox Sign.
- PandaDoc's content library/"Forms" and Docusign's "Agreement Prep" were not examined in detail for profile-like variable reuse. Docusign also has a "Custom fields/data labels" feature that may approximate a profile; not verified.
- Whether pdfFiller's "AI Form Filler" uses stored profile data or only in-document context is unknown.

## 5. What gaps could a newcomer promise on its website?

### Takeaway
Based on documented competitor failures, credible differentiators for ShipShape are:
- no early-termination fees, trial reminders, and one-click cancel (exceeding the Adobe order)
- no "free trial" that silently becomes an annual charge (pdfFiller, Smallpdf)
- an honest free plan without send/save paywalls after completion (pdfFiller Android report)
- a contractual "no training on your documents — by us or our AI providers, no anonymized-aggregate exception" (vs Docusign consent-to-train, PandaDoc AI Addendum, Jotform contradictions)
- a deletable, user-confirmed profile (vs Adobe's sticky autofill)
- no per-envelope or per-signature caps that punish repeat senders
- human support for billing even on free or cancelled plans (vs Docusign)

### Cited Findings
- The Adobe order's required behaviors: disclose ETFs upfront, remind before trial conversion, easy cancel — [DOJ press release, Mar 2026](https://business.cch.com/ald/OfficeofPublicAffairs_AdobeAgreesto150MillionSettlementandInjunctiontoResolveAllegedViolationsoftheRestoreOnlineShoppersConfidenceAct_UnitedStatesDepartmentofJustice032026.pdf)
- Paywall-after-completion pattern (pdfFiller Android, 2023 report) — [Deceptive Design](https://deceptive.design/articles/airslate-inc-pdffiller-requires-subscription-after-document-completion-and-free-trial-still-asks-for-cc-info)
- Trial-to-annual charges without reminder (Smallpdf) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/smallpdf.com?page=4)
- Docusign trains on consenting customers' anonymized aggregate data — [Docusign AI Trust](https://www.docusign.com/en-gb/products/platform/ai/ai-trust)
- PandaDoc's AI Addendum asserts perpetual rights over "AI Improvement Data" — [PandaDoc AI Addendum](https://www.pandadoc.com/legal/ai-addendum/)
- Jotform's AI Policy is internally inconsistent on training — [Jotform AI Policy](https://www.jotform.com/ai-policy/)
- Docusign: free and cancelled users can't open support cases — [Docusign Community 10585](https://community.docusign.com/questions/10585)
- Envelope, signature and template caps — [PandaDoc blog on Docusign](https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/docusign-pricing/); [Jotform Answers](https://www.jotform.com/answers/40282401-signature-limit-on-starter-plan); [eversign on Dropbox Sign](https://eversign.com/blog/dropbox-sign-essentials-vs-standard-plans)
- Adobe's forced terms re-acceptance and "unclear" license wording destroyed trust even though Adobe said it didn't train on content — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2024/06/no-ai-training-in-newly-distrusted-terms-of-service-adobe-says)
- Dropbox's default-on third-party AI toggle (2023) — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/12/36298097/dropbox-ceo-denies-sharing-user-data-with-openai-but-the-option-to-disable-ai-search-is-now-hidden)
- Signer data exposed in the Dropbox Sign breach — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2024/05/dropbox-sign-customer-data-accessed-in-breach)

### Inferences
Candidate website promises, each mapped to a competitor failure:
1. "Cancel in one click, anytime. No early-termination fees. We email you 3 days before any trial or renewal charge." (Adobe ROSCA order; Smallpdf and pdfFiller trial complaints)
2. "Free means free. Fill, sign and download a clean PDF on the free plan with no card required. Paid plans add volume and features, not your own finished document." (pdfFiller paywall-after-completion)
3. "We never train AI on your documents or profile, and neither do our AI providers. No 'anonymized aggregate' exception. No license over AI outputs." (Docusign, PandaDoc Addendum, Jotform)
4. "Terms changes in plain English, with a diff, and never forced via a blocking pop-up." (Adobe 2024)
5. "Your business profile is yours. We show you what we remember, ask you to re-confirm it, and delete it on request." (Adobe autofill persistence complaints)
6. "No per-envelope caps on sending your own standard packet." (Docusign, Jotform, Dropbox Sign caps)
7. "AI features are off until you turn them on." (Dropbox 2023 default-on toggle; Docusign consent ambiguity)
8. "Billing support for everyone, including free and cancelled accounts." (Docusign support paywall)

Promises 1, 3 and 6 must be fully honored operationally. A broken "no dark patterns" promise is a ROSCA/FTC Act deception risk in its own right.

### Gaps
- No direct customer-survey data was found quantifying which promise matters most to SMBs.
- Did not check whether any competitor already markets "no training" prominently on its homepage. Smallpdf's terms commit to it, but its marketing prominence is unknown.
