# ShipShape Marketing Site: What Makes a SaaS Site Look Professional and Trustworthy (as of Oct 2026)

Scope: planning only. ShipShape is a US web app for small businesses (startups, freelancers, small businesses, contractors). It remembers business facts, finds official forms, fills and edits PDFs, drafts documents with warnings, and shares a "paperwork passport" link. Positioning: honest billing, no AI training on customer documents, privacy-first, not a law firm. Look: black-and-white editorial (NYT or magazine) with Claude-like simplicity.

Research method: I fetched the live homepages of linear.app, mercury.com, stripe.com, anthropic.com, legalzoom.com and rocketlawyer.com, plus linear.app/security, in October 2026. Regulatory, research and benchmark facts come from web searches; most results were law-firm or secondary summaries, and primary sources are linked where they appeared. Not fetched because of the tool budget: claude.ai, ramp.com, notion.com, gusto.com and the Anthropic trust center (see Gaps).

## 1. Benchmarks: how leading sites structure home pages, and which elements correlate with trust and conversion

### Takeaway
The best sites share one skeleton:
1. A one-line declarative hero with one or two CTAs.
2. Immediate proof: logos, hard numbers and named-person quotes.
3. A product tour of three to six capability blocks, each linking to a deeper page.
4. A closing CTA.
5. A dense footer that always links Security, Status, Changelog, Docs/Help and Legal (Privacy, Terms, DPA).

Regulated or quasi-legal brands (Mercury, Stripe, LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer) add plain-language "we are not a bank / law firm" disclosures. Research on trust (NN/g) emphasizes design quality, upfront disclosure (especially pricing), current comprehensive content and outside validation. Conversion research (Unbounce) favors easier-to-read pages with fewer words.

**Trust and conversion research**
- NN/g's trust framework (Nielsen, originally 1999, re-validated in a later NN/g cross-cultural study) has four drivers: design quality, upfront disclosure of all customer-relevant information (pricing, policies), comprehensive and current content, and connection to the rest of the web (reviews, associations). Typos and broken links quickly degrade credibility. Note that the framework is old in origin, and I saw search-result fragments rather than the full article. — [NN/g: Trustworthy design](https://nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- A Stanford web credibility study found 46.1% of consumers judged credibility partly on visual design appeal (an older study, early 2000s). — cited via [PMC review: Credibility judgments in web page design](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4863498/)
- Unbounce benchmark (2024 edition): the median SaaS landing-page conversion rate is 3.8%, 42% below the 6.6% all-industry median. Another Unbounce page gives 2.9% for SaaS without naming the edition, so the two figures conflict. — [Unbounce SaaS conversion benchmark](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)
- Unbounce: SaaS conversion rates rise as reading ease improves and fall as word count rises. The older 2020 report called SaaS pages "the most difficult to read" of all industries analyzed. — [Unbounce SaaS benchmark](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/); [Unbounce 2020 report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report-2020/)

### Inferences
- Every benchmark site has the same footer "trust spine": Security, Status, Changelog, Docs/Help and Legal (Privacy, Terms, DPA). For ShipShape it is a cheap, high-signal requirement on day one.
- Mercury's and Stripe's "not a bank" lines are the closest model for ShipShape's "not a law firm" disclosure. They state it plainly in or near the hero or relevant section, not only in fine print. LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer bury it in the footer.
  - Recommendation: a short in-context line near the hero or product tour ("ShipShape is software, not a law firm. We don't give legal advice."), the full disclosure in the footer, and a dedicated /not-a-law-firm page.
- Rocket Lawyer's mismatched prices and user counts are exactly the small inconsistencies NN/g says erode trust, and an anti-pattern for an "honest billing" brand. Every number on the ShipShape site should come from one source of truth.
- The Anthropic and Stripe editorial modules (dated cards, a "book of the week") show that editorial layouts and commercial pages can coexist. This validates the NYT or magazine direction.
- Recommended home page order for ShipShape:
  1. A declarative serif hero (one sentence plus one subhead) with "Start free" and "See a sample passport," the latter as a live demo link.
  2. A three-part "what it does" row: Remembers your facts / Finds the right official form / Fills it, with warnings.
  3. A real product screenshot or short interactive tour.
  4. A "Paperwork passport" explainer.
  5. A "Promises" block: honest billing, no training on your documents, privacy-first, not a law firm. Each promise links to its proof page.
  6. Social proof: named small-business customers, only once real.
  7. A pricing teaser with the actual price.
  8. A "From the Journal" editorial strip.
  9. A closing CTA.
  10. The footer trust spine.

### Gaps
- claude.ai, ramp.com, notion.com and gusto.com homepages were not fetched (tool budget), so I can't report their current structures.
- I found no Baymard or CXL study, in this session, quantifying the conversion effect of specific SaaS trust elements (security badges, logo bars). Baymard's work is mostly e-commerce checkout. Do not cite specific "trust badge lifts conversion X%" claims without a primary source.
- The full NN/g article text was not retrieved. The four-factor summary comes from search excerpts.

## 2. Trust pages: security, subprocessors, plain-language privacy, AI/data use, status, "not a law firm"

### Takeaway
Best practice is a security page that summarizes concrete controls (certifications, encryption specifics, identity features, privacy features). It links a trust portal for audit reports (SOC 2 or ISO on request) and a separate subprocessor list, with a DPA, status page and responsible-disclosure policy linked from the footer. For an AI document product, a dedicated "AI & your data" page and a "not a law firm" page are the differentiators.

### Cited Findings
- Linear's security page has three sections: "Enterprise-grade security" (SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, GDPR, HIPAA with a BAA request form, audit logs, admin controls), "Identity management" (SSO, passkeys, SAML, SCIM, domain claiming, IP restrictions) and "Privacy" (private teams, guest accounts, encryption, multi-region hosting). — [linear.app/security](https://linear.app/security)
- Linear states its encryption specifics: HTTPS forced, TLS 1.2 in transit, AES-256 at rest. Audit reports are gated behind a request on a separate trust portal (trust.linear.app). — [linear.app/security](https://linear.app/security)
- The Linear security page itself doesn't show a subprocessor list or a vulnerability-disclosure policy. The DPA is only a footer link. Even best-in-class sites spread this across pages. — [linear.app/security](https://linear.app/security)
- Anthropic puts "Trust center (Security and compliance)" and "Transparency" under a top-level "Commitments" nav item. The footer links a responsible disclosure policy, usage policy, privacy policy, consumer health data privacy policy and status. — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com)
- Vanta Trust Center features (per Vanta help docs):
  - admins choose which controls to show (only passing, or all)
  - a Subprocessors tab (the first four on the front page, the rest on the tab)
  - visit and access-request analytics
  - an API for subprocessor data — [Vanta help: Trust Center](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/11345469-vanta-trust-center); [Vanta help: Adding subprocessors](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/11345937-adding-subprocessors-to-vanta-trust-centers)
- A third-party blog claims the Vanta Trust Center pulls "live" data from monitoring agents. Vanta's own docs don't confirm this. — [ComplyJet (secondary)](https://www.complyjet.com/blog/vanta-trust-center)
- "Not a law firm" disclosure examples:
  - LegalZoom: "is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice, except where authorized through its subsidiary law firm…" — [legalzoom.com](https://www.legalzoom.com)
  - Rocket Lawyer: "not a lawyer referral service… or law firm," "does not provide legal representation." — [rocketlawyer.com](https://www.rocketlawyer.com)
  - Both place it in the footer only.
- Mercury is the in-context model for a regulatory disclosure: "Mercury is a fintech company, not an FDIC-insured bank…" sits next to the deposit-insurance claim it qualifies. — [mercury.com](https://mercury.com)
- DoNotPay ("robot lawyer") was sanctioned by the FTC partly because it claimed lawyer-equivalent capability. The proposed order required notifying 2021–2023 subscribers about the limits of its law-related features. A clear "not a law firm" boundary is risk mitigation, not just a nicety. — [Orrick summary of Operation AI Comply](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/10/FTC-Targets-Unfair-or-Deceptive-AI-Practices-With-Five-New-Enforcement-Actions)

### Inferences
Recommended ShipShape trust cluster under a top-level "Trust" or "Promises" nav item, modeled on Anthropic's "Commitments":

**/trust (overview)**
- The four promises, each with a one-paragraph plain summary and a "read the details" link.
- A "last updated" date.
- A changelog of policy changes.

**/security**
- Encryption specifics (state actual algorithms and TLS versions).
- Hosting region (US).
- Access controls (passkeys, 2FA).
- Employee access policy ("staff can't view your documents without your ticketed permission").
- Backup and deletion timelines.
- Responsible disclosure contact (security.txt).
- Certifications: SOC 2 status, stated honestly as "in progress" if true. Do not display a badge before the audit completes.

**/subprocessors**
- A table: vendor, purpose, data categories, location, date added.
- An email subscription for changes.

**/ai (AI & your data)**
- Which model providers are used.
- A contractual no-training commitment for customer documents.
- Retention at the AI provider.
- What the AI can't do: it isn't a lawyer, it can be wrong, and the user reviews before filing.
- How warnings are generated.

**/privacy**
- A plain-language summary box ("The short version") at the top, then the full policy.
- US state privacy rights section.

**/not-a-law-firm**
- What ShipShape is (software that helps you find and fill forms) and isn't (it gives no legal advice and creates no attorney-client relationship).
- When to see a lawyer, with links to state bar referral services.
- Display it in context near drafting features and in the footer.

**Elsewhere**
- /status: hosted on a separate domain or subdomain, as Linear does.
- /changelog: dated entries.

Because the brand promise is privacy, the trust pages are product pages for ShipShape, not legal boilerplate. They deserve the same editorial typography as the home page.

### Gaps
- The Anthropic trust center (trust.anthropic.com) wasn't fetched, so its exact structure isn't described here.
- I didn't find an authoritative source on bar-association rules (unauthorized practice of law) governing disclaimer wording for document-prep software. The exact wording of ShipShape's disclaimer should go to counsel.
- No primary research found that quantifies conversion lift from trust pages.

## 3. Pricing page best practices: honest billing, auto-renewal law, FTC, Adobe

### Takeaway
The "honest billing" brand position has teeth in 2026:
- The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025, but ROSCA, Section 5 of the FTC Act and state auto-renewal laws still apply.
- California's expanded ARL (AB 2863, effective July 1, 2025) requires express affirmative consent, annual reminders and same-medium cancellation.
- Adobe's $150M DOJ/FTC settlement (proposed March 2026) centers on early-termination fees disclosed only at cancellation.

ShipShape's pricing page should show the real total price, the billing cadence, and the renewal and cancellation terms before signup.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC click-to-cancel rule:** The Negative Option ("click-to-cancel") Rule was adopted in October 2024. The Eighth Circuit vacated it entirely on July 8, 2025, in *Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC*, No. 24-3137, on procedural grounds: the FTC skipped a preliminary regulatory analysis despite projected costs over $100M. — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule)
- After the vacatur, businesses remain subject to ROSCA, state auto-renewal laws, Section 5 of the FTC Act and state UDAP laws. — [Gunderson Dettmer](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)
- **California ARL (AB 2863, Chapter 515, Statutes of 2024):**
  - effective July 1, 2025 per law-firm alerts (one news source said Jan 1, 2025, a conflict; the law-firm date is more reliable)
  - requires "express affirmative consent" to auto-renewal terms, with consent records kept 3 years or 1 year after termination, whichever is longer
  - requires an annual reminder stating the product, the frequency and amount of charges, and how to cancel
  - requires cancellation in the same medium as signup (online signup means online cancellation)
  - now covers free-to-paid conversions — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect); [CalMatters Digital Democracy: AB 2863](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/CA_202320240AB2863)
- **Adobe (March 2026):** a proposed settlement totaling $150M ($75M civil penalties plus $75M in free services). Adobe denies wrongdoing. — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/adobe-ends-federal-subscription-lawsuit-with-150-million-settlement-package); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/adobe-reaches-usd150m-settlement-on-us-lawsuit-over-alleged-hidden-subscription-fees-cancellation-charges); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/03/19/adobe-settles-us-case-over-cancel-terms/)
  - The government alleged that Adobe's early termination fee was 50% of the remaining first-year monthly payments, disclosed only when subscribers tried to cancel. — [FTC complaint (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/032-RedactedComplaint-REPLACEMENT.pdf)
  - The proposed order requires Adobe to clearly disclose any ETF and how it is calculated before enrollment, to notify free-trial users (trials over 7 days) before converting them to a paid plan with an ETF, and to provide simple cancellation.
  - Status: proposed. The results didn't confirm court entry as of this research. — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/adobe-ends-federal-subscription-lawsuit-with-150-million-settlement-package)
- **Real-world anti-pattern:** Rocket Lawyer's homepage shows annual plans as monthly equivalents ($12.41/mo, "Billed yearly after free 7-day trial"), while its FAQ lists different monthly prices ($34.99 and others). It has no explicit auto-renew disclosure beyond the billing line. — [rocketlawyer.com](https://www.rocketlawyer.com)
- **Positive pattern:** Stripe states "Integrated per-transaction pricing with no hidden fees" on the homepage. — [stripe.com](https://stripe.com)
- **Hidden pricing (NN/g, via secondary summaries):** In NN/g studies, users who can't find prices leave for competitors' sites. B2B customers rank pricing as the most-needed information. NN/g calls hidden pricing a "hostile" experience. The original article wasn't retrieved. — [Populi summary of NN/g "Show your pricing"](https://populiweb.com/blog/2013/12/nielsen-norman-group-show-your-pricing/) (2013, older)

### Inferences
Recommended ShipShape pricing page:
- **Monthly and annual side by side, not a toggle that hides one.** Show the annual total ("$X billed once per year") as the headline number, with the monthly equivalent secondary. Never show only "$/mo" for annual plans.
- **A plain "How billing works" box above the fold**, covering:
  - when you're charged
  - renewal date
  - the reminder email before annual renewal (meets California's annual-notice requirement and goes further)
  - "Cancel online in two clicks from Settings → Billing"
  - prorated refund policy
  - no early termination fees, stated explicitly as an anti-Adobe signal
- **Free trial:** no card required if feasible. If a card is required, state the conversion date and amount at signup and send a reminder before conversion. Since AB 2863 covers free-to-paid conversions, use an unchecked consent checkbox next to the renewal terms.
- **No dark patterns:**
  - no pre-checked add-ons
  - no "Most popular" badge unless it is true
  - no fake countdowns or urgency
  - no "contact us" for self-serve tiers
- **A public "Billing promises" page**, linked from pricing and the footer, plus a pricing changelog: "we'll give 30 days' notice of price changes; existing annual terms are honored."
- **A cost-comparison note** may be useful, e.g. "Is ShipShape cheaper than a lawyer? No; it's a different thing." Keep comparisons factual and substantiated.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether the FTC began new negative-option rulemaking after the vacatur (2025–2026). Check FTC press releases.
- I did not verify whether a court entered the Adobe order.
- The AB 2863 summary relies on law-firm alerts, not the statute text (Bus. & Prof. Code §17600 et seq.). Counsel should confirm the exact disclosure placement requirements (e.g. "clear and conspicuous" proximity to the CTA).
- Other state auto-renewal laws (NY, IL, CO, MN and others) were not researched.

## 4. Showing an AI product credibly: demos, screenshots, AI claims, testimonials

### Takeaway
Show the real product (real screenshots, a live or sample "passport," an interactive tour), state limitations openly, and avoid superlative or lawyer-equivalence claims. The FTC treats "AI" claims as substantiable, not puffery, and Operation AI Comply specifically hit a "robot lawyer." The 2024 FTC rule bans fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials, with civil penalties per violation.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC "Keep Your AI Claims in Check"** (FTC Business Blog, Feb 27, 2023, older but still the core guidance). It asks:
  - Are you exaggerating what your AI product can do?
  - Are you promising it does something better than a non-AI product, and can you prove it?
  - Are you aware of the risks?
  - Does the product actually use AI?

  Claims must be accurate, substantiated and disclose material limitations. "AI" isn't treated as puffery. — [Kelley Drye summary](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-reminds-advertisers-to-keep-ai-claims-in-check); [Cooley summary](https://cdp.cooley.com/ftc-warns-to-keep-your-ai-claims-in-check-in-new-ai-guidance/)
- **Operation AI Comply** (announced Sept 25, 2024): five enforcement actions. Chair Lina Khan stated there is "no AI exemption from the laws on the books." — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/10/FTC-Targets-Unfair-or-Deceptive-AI-Practices-With-Five-New-Enforcement-Actions)
  - DoNotPay advertised a "robot lawyer" and claimed it could help users "sue for assault without a lawyer." The FTC alleged it never tested whether its output matched a human lawyer's and did not hire attorneys. The proposed settlement was $193,000 plus a notice to 2021–2023 subscribers about the limitations. — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/10/FTC-Targets-Unfair-or-Deceptive-AI-Practices-With-Five-New-Enforcement-Actions); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ftc-deceptive-ai-project-crackdown-inclues-ai-lawyer)
  - Rytr, an AI writing tool, was barred from offering services that generate consumer reviews or testimonials. — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/10/FTC-Targets-Unfair-or-Deceptive-AI-Practices-With-Five-New-Enforcement-Actions)
- **FTC fake reviews and testimonials rule** (finalized Aug 2024, effective 60 days after Federal Register publication). — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-final-rule-targeting-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials-including-those-generated-by-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-online-bots)
  - It bans creating, buying or selling fake consumer or celebrity reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones.
  - It bans suppressing negative reviews through threats or intimidation.
  - It covers inadequately disclosed company-controlled review sites and fake social-media indicators.
  - Penalties are up to about $51,744 per violation (inflation-adjusted annually; check the current figure), possibly counted per fake review.
- **How benchmark sites show product and proof:**
  - Linear uses a real product screenshot in the hero. — [linear.app](https://linear.app)
  - Mercury offers "Launch demo," an interactive demo with no signup, as a hero CTA. — [mercury.com](https://mercury.com)
  - Rocket Lawyer makes the AI prompt box the hero, with sample questions. — [rocketlawyer.com](https://www.rocketlawyer.com)
  - Stripe embeds a guided "Get recommendations" form. — [stripe.com](https://stripe.com)
  - Testimonials across Linear, Mercury and Stripe carry full names, titles and companies. — [linear.app](https://linear.app); [mercury.com](https://mercury.com); [stripe.com](https://stripe.com)

### Inferences
- **Claims to avoid:**
  - "AI lawyer," "legal advice," "lawyer-quality"
  - "never makes mistakes," "100% accurate"
  - "guaranteed compliance," "saves X hours" (unless measured with a documented method)
  - "the smartest…"
- **Claims to prefer (verifiable and modest):**
  - "Finds the official form from the agency's own website and links you to the source."
  - "Flags common problems before you file. You review and sign."
- **Show the warnings.** A screenshot of a draft with ShipShape's warning callouts is itself a trust signal: it demonstrates honesty about limits, the opposite of the DoNotPay posture.
- **Demo format ranking for ShipShape:**
  1. A public sample "paperwork passport" for a fictional business, clearly labeled as a sample (the share link is the product's most distinctive artifact).
  2. A click-through interactive tour built from real UI. Label it "Product tour," not a simulation of results.
  3. Real screenshots in a neutral black-and-white frame.
  4. Avoid illustrated "AI magic" imagery (sparkles, glowing brains). It conflicts with the editorial look and reads as hype.
- **Testimonials:** use only real, attributable customers who consented (name, business type, city). Disclose any incentive (free months). Don't use AI to write or "polish" quotes. Don't seed with team or friends without disclosure. Until real customers exist, use founder notes or "design partner" quotes with disclosure, or no testimonials.
- **Every sample in the demo should be fictional and labeled as such**, e.g. "Sample business: Juniper Plumbing LLC (fictional)." Do not use real agency seals or logos in a way that implies endorsement.

### Gaps
- I didn't confirm the final status of the DoNotPay order (the Jan/Feb 2025 FTC finalization isn't confirmed in this session).
- No 2025–2026 FTC AI-advertising guidance was found beyond the 2023 blog and the 2024 sweep. Check ftc.gov for newer AI-claims cases.
- No conversion research was found comparing interactive demos with video or screenshots for SaaS.

## 5. Performance and accessibility: Core Web Vitals, WCAG 2.2 AA, typography

### Takeaway
Targets: LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms and CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile of real users (CrUX field data). WCAG 2.2 AA is the sensible conformance target in 2026, even though US Title II regulation references WCAG 2.1 AA. A text-first black-and-white editorial site is naturally well suited to both, if web fonts and sticky headers are handled carefully.

### Cited Findings
- **Core Web Vitals thresholds:**
  - "Good": LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1.
  - "Poor": LCP >4.0s, INP >500ms, CLS >0.25.
  - Assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads. — [web.dev: Defining Core Web Vitals thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)
  - Google ranks on field data from CrUX (Chrome real-user data). Lighthouse lab data is only a proxy. — [Unlighthouse (secondary)](https://unlighthouse.dev/learn-lighthouse/core-web-vitals.md); [ppc.land (secondary)](https://ppc.land/core-web-vitals/)
  - No change to these thresholds for 2026 was found.
- **WCAG 2.2 additions:** nine new success criteria, and 4.1.1 Parsing removed. — [WebAbility (secondary)](https://www.webability.io/blog/wcag-2-2-vs-wcag-2-1); [University of Arizona: WCAG 2.2 highlights](https://accessibility.arizona.edu/policies-governance/wcag-22-highlights)
  - New criteria include 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (AA), target size, dragging alternatives, consistent help and accessible authentication.
  - Focus Not Obscured is commonly failed by sticky headers and cookie banners covering the focused element.
- **Legal status (conflicting sources):**
  - Two sources say the DOJ's ADA Title II rule references WCAG 2.1 AA. — [University of Arizona](https://accessibility.arizona.edu/policies-governance/wcag-22-highlights)
  - One vendor blog claims WCAG 2.2 AA became the legal benchmark in January 2026. This is unverified and likely wrong. — [TestParty (vendor)](https://testparty.ai/blog/wcag-2-2-us-companies-2026)
  - Title II applies to state and local governments, not to private SaaS. Private businesses face ADA Title III litigation, where plaintiffs increasingly cite WCAG 2.2 (the specific lawsuit-percentage claims are vendor-sourced and unverified).

### Inferences
- **Editorial black-and-white design and WCAG:**
  - Pure black on white passes contrast easily.
  - Watch gray secondary text (needs ≥4.5:1 for body, ≥3:1 for large text) and thin serif weights at small sizes.
  - Never use color alone for warnings. Use icons plus the label "Warning."
- **Sticky header and cookie banner:** these must not cover focused elements (2.4.11). Use `scroll-padding-top` equal to the header height.
- **Target size:** WCAG 2.2 AA 2.5.8 (24×24 CSS px minimum) affects small footer links and pricing toggles. (This criterion detail is from general knowledge of the spec; verify at w3.org.)
- **Accessible authentication (3.3.8):** the signup and login pages linked from the marketing site should allow paste and password managers, with no cognitive puzzles. Passkeys are good.
- **Performance:**
  - Self-host one or two variable fonts (a serif display face and a sans or serif text face), subset them, preload the hero font and use `font-display: swap` with metric-matched fallbacks to protect CLS.
  - Serve hero screenshots as AVIF or WebP with explicit width and height.
  - Avoid heavy client JS on marketing pages (prefer static or SSG), which keeps INP low.
  - Load interactive demos lazily, below the fold or on click.
- **Long-form readability:** roughly 60–75 characters per line, 16–20px body text, line-height around 1.5, generous paragraph spacing. These are conventional typographic norms, not sourced in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve the W3C WCAG 2.2 spec text directly. Criterion numbers and levels beyond 2.4.11 come from my general knowledge and secondary sources, so verify at w3.org/TR/WCAG22.
- No authoritative 2025–2026 source on readability metrics (line length, font size) was retrieved. NN/g and Baymard have relevant articles that weren't fetched.
- I didn't verify whether Google changed any CWV metric or threshold in 2026. None was found.

## 6. Content and SEO: resource library, form guides, editorial "journal"

### Takeaway
Editorial content is a proven trust and acquisition layer for finance and legal-adjacent SaaS:
- Stripe puts Stripe Press and a "What's happening" editorial strip on its commercial homepage.
- LegalZoom runs a large SEO "Learn more" library of how-to explainers.
- Ramp is hiring for daily content shipping and "LLM results" optimization.

For ShipShape, a "Form Guides" library (one page per official form or filing) plus an NYT-style "Journal" is the natural fit, as long as content stays accurate, dated and reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe's homepage includes a "Book of the week" block linking to Stripe Press and Works in Progress, plus news cards (annual letter, BFCM stats). The footer lists Guides, Blog and Stripe Press. — [stripe.com](https://stripe.com)
- Secondary analysis credits Stripe with treating documentation as a marketing asset. It says Stripe layered Atlas and Press on top of its content to build founder trust, and organized the site around product hubs by use case and industry to build topical authority. These are secondary and opinion-level claims. — [Lilach Bullock: Stripe marketing strategy](https://www.lilachbullock.com/stripe-marketing-strategy/); [Omnius: Stripe SEO](https://www.omnius.so/blog/stripe-seo)
- Ramp job postings describe:
  - an SEO operations role focused on "SEO and LLM results," using generative-AI content workflows and programmatic content vendors, with a target of "shipping new and updated content daily"
  - content marketing aimed at its "small business and startup customer base," covering product announcements to how-to guides — [Ramp SEO Ops posting](https://jobs.generalcatalyst.com/companies/ramp-2/jobs/45122122-seo-operations-manager); [Ramp content marketing posting](https://startup.jobs/content-marketing-manager-ramp-3314588)
- LegalZoom's footer "Learn more" list targets high-intent informational queries: "What is an LLC?", "How to Start an LLC?", "What Is a Registered Agent?", "How to Find Out if a Business Name is Taken?" It also has a Business Name Generator tool page and legal form templates. — [legalzoom.com](https://www.legalzoom.com)
- Mercury's footer lists Resources: Help Center, Blog, Tools, Events. — [mercury.com](https://mercury.com)
- Anthropic's homepage is effectively a publication front page: dated cards, categories, research and policy essays. — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com)

### Inferences

**Recommended site map**
```
/                       Home
/product                How it works (Memory · Find forms · Fill & edit PDFs · Draft with warnings · Passport)
  /product/passport     The Paperwork Passport (+ public sample passport)
/for/                   Startups · Freelancers · Small businesses · Contractors (use-case pages)
/pricing                Side-by-side monthly/annual, billing box, FAQ
/trust                  Promises overview
  /security  /privacy (plain-language summary + full)  /ai (AI & your data)
  /subprocessors  /billing-promises  /not-a-law-firm  /accessibility
/forms                  Form Guides library (by agency/state/topic)
  /forms/irs-w-9, /forms/irs-ss-4, /forms/state/ca/statement-of-information ...
/journal                Editorial: essays, explainers, "Deadlines this month", interviews
/changelog              Dated product updates
/help                   Help center / docs
/about  /contact  /careers(optional)  /press
status.shipshape.(tld)  Status page
/legal/terms  /legal/dpa  /legal/aup
```

**Form Guides template** (the highest SEO and trust value). Each guide includes:
- the official form name and number
- the issuing agency, with a link to the official source
- "who needs it" and "when it's due"
- what information you'll need
- common mistakes
- a "Last checked against the official source: [date]" line and a named reviewer or editor
- a CTA: "Fill this with ShipShape"
- the not-a-law-firm note

Dated "last verified" lines directly address NN/g's "current content" trust factor.

**Journal (NYT or magazine style):**
- Bylined, dated pieces in a few clear sections: Explainers, Deadlines, Field Notes (interviews with real small-business owners), Policy (privacy and AI stances).
- Serif headlines, drop caps optional, pull quotes, a restrained black-and-white photo or illustration style.
- A "Deadlines this month" recurring column suits the audience and builds return visits.

**Quality guardrails:**
- Avoid mass-produced programmatic or AI-written pages. They are risky for a legal-adjacent brand and contradict the "honest" positioning.
- Every guide should cite the official source.
- Ramp's daily-content approach is a scale model, not necessarily a fit for an early-stage brand whose differentiator is accuracy.

**Homepage tie-in:** a "From the Journal" strip with two or three dated cards (Stripe and Anthropic pattern).

- No primary data on Stripe Press's measurable effect on brand trust or acquisition. Claims are from secondary commentary.
- Search guidance on AI-generated content and "helpful content" in 2026 wasn't researched.
